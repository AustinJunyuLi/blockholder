\section{Interpretation of Possible Findings}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The two primary behavioural margins are the response of campaign entry and
composition to required-trading-days exposure and the response of Item~6
derivative use. Table~\ref{tab:findings} states the interpretation of each
combination without presuming an outcome.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{Interpretation of the primary empirical margins}
\label{tab:findings}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.33\linewidth}p{0.33\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
& No increase in Item~6 derivatives & Increase in Item~6 derivatives \\
\midrule
Entry or composition responds to exposure
& Campaign supply or target composition changes without detectable derivative
substitution.
& Campaign activity changes and derivative use rises in exposed targets. \\
No detectable entry or composition response
& No detectable behavioural response within the powered range.
& Derivative use rises without a detectable change in campaign supply or target
composition. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Subject to the identification checks, a campaign response without derivative
substitution validates the chilling channel in direction, though not
necessarily in magnitude. A joint response links the accumulation constraint
to the regulatory treatment of derivatives. An increase in derivative use
without a campaign response would identify the decision not to adopt proposed
Rule~13d-3(e), rather than the headline deadline change, as the operative policy
margin. No response on either margin is the modal ex ante outcome given the
SEC's compliance statistics and would provide a policy null bounded by the
reported confidence intervals and minimum detectable effects.

These interpretations remain conditional on the pre-trend, placebo, and
bundled-reform checks. A null estimate does not establish that the reform had no
effect outside the detectable range. Conversely, a response in a mechanically
affected timing measure does not establish a behavioural effect.

The gain decomposition addresses a separate measurement question. It identifies
whether returns associated with activist targets are realised at filing, during
the pre-bid run-up, or in the bid markup. A lower markup is not by itself
evidence of a lower total gain. The relevant test considers all three components
over a fixed trigger-to-bid horizon and distinguishes a redistribution of gains
across event time from a change in total target-shareholder value.
