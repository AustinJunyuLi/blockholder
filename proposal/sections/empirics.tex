\section{Empirical Design and Data Acquisition}

This design implements the identification matrix and data-feasibility findings of the E1 and
E2 memos rather than the pivot memo's original activist-level designs. The headline test is the
chilling/substitution result: entry and composition dose-response on a predetermined
target-level exposure measure, paired with Item 6 cash-settled-derivative substitution through
the hole the Commission left open when it declined to adopt proposed Rule 13d-3(e). The
event-time-invariant anatomy of activist target gains is the second act. Capitalization is a
measurement lemma inside that anatomy, not a headline: \textcite{celentanolevine2025} own the
capitalization interpretation and \textcite{bett2014} own the substitution evidence this design
adjudicates against realized post-reform data.

\subsection{Setting and Identification Stance}

The reform is a single adoption event. Rule 13d-1(a) was amended to shorten the initial
Schedule 13D filing deadline from ten calendar days to five business days, effective February 5,
2024 \parencite{sec2023modernization}. There are no never-treated units: every initial 13D
filer is subject to the same deadline after that date, so identification runs entirely through
continuous treatment intensity rather than a treated/control contrast. Let $x$ denote the
activist's eventual stake (fraction of shares outstanding), $L$ target liquidity (average daily
volume over the six months ending 60 trading days before the trigger, or its Amihud
illiquidity companion), $\phi$ the maximum
share of $L$ tradable per day without alerting the market, and $d$ the legal filing window in
business days ($d \approx 7$ pre-reform, $d = 5$ post-reform). Define the exposure measure

\[
\text{RTD}_i \;=\; \frac{0.05 \times S_{\mathrm{out},i}}{\phi \times \mathrm{ADV}_i},
\]

with $S_{\mathrm{out},i}$ shares outstanding: the number of trading days required to move five
percentage points of ownership at the participation cap. The statutory clock starts when the
activist crosses the 5\% threshold; over the following $d$ business days at most
$\phi \times \mathrm{ADV}_i \times d$ shares can be added, so the window permits an undisclosed
increment of at least another five percentage points iff $\text{RTD}_i \le d$. The binary
exposure is $\mathbf{1}\{\text{RTD}_i > d\}$ and the continuous dose is $\text{RTD}_i/d$, the
five-percentage-point increment being the stated normalisation. Because $\text{RTD}_i$ is built
from pre-trigger,
predetermined target characteristics, it is defined for every filing, including one-time filers,
and does not depend on the campaign's realized outcome. Because the untimed pre-trigger phase
can extend into any finite measurement window, the ADV window is lagged (six months ending 60
trading days before the trigger), residual own-trading in it is diagnosed from the Item~5(c)
sixty-day transaction tables, and every specification is re-estimated with a 120-trading-day
lag --- extending the caution of \textcite{collindufresnefos2015} from controls to the
exposure measure itself.

\begin{assumption}[Continuous-dose parallel trends]
In the absence of the reform, outcomes for targets at every level of $\text{RTD}_i$ would have
evolved along parallel paths, and the dose-response function is stable across the exposure
distribution over the estimation window.
\end{assumption}

Under this assumption a single interaction coefficient from

\[
Y_{i,q} \;=\; \alpha_{a(i),q} + \gamma_{\ell(i),q} + \beta \,\text{Post}_q \times \text{RTD}_i +
\varepsilon_{i,q}
\]

identifies an average causal response only if the dose-response is homogeneous; if it is not,
$\beta$ is a weighted average of heterogeneous responses whose weights need not be
well-behaved. We therefore treat $\beta$ as a summary statistic only and report the primary
result nonparametrically,

\[
Y_{i,q} \;=\; \alpha_{a(i),q} + \gamma_{\ell(i),q} +
\sum_{k} \beta_k \,\mathbf{1}[\text{RTD-bin}_i = k]\times \text{Post}_q + \varepsilon_{i,q},
\]

across exposure bins, with $\alpha_{a(i),q}$ an activist$\times$quarter fixed effect and
$\gamma_{\ell(i),q}$ a liquidity-decile$\times$quarter fixed effect (subsection 2).

\paragraph{Anticipation and the double donut.} Anticipation predates the effective date by two
years: the amendments were proposed and published at 87 FR 13846 (Mar.\ 10, 2022), the comment
period was reopened Apr.\ 28, 2023, and the release was adopted (dated) October 10, 2023, four
months before the February 5, 2024 effective date \parencite{sec2023modernization}. We donut
out the October 2023--February 2024 window from any pre-period, and separately test robustness
to donuts around the March 2022 proposal and the April 2023 reopening, since either could have
partially treated behavior ahead of the effective date.

\paragraph{Bundling defense.} Release 33-11253 moved four things at once: it shortened the
initial 13D deadline to five business days; it shortened Schedule 13D amendments to two
business days; it restructured every Schedule 13G filing and amendment deadline; and it
mandated XML-based structured filing for Schedules 13D and 13G, with compliance not required
until December 18, 2024 \parencite{sec2023modernization}. Separately, and centrally to the
substitution channel, the release declined to adopt proposed Rule 13d-3(e) on cash-settled
derivative securities, instead amending Item 6 to clarify (without expanding) disclosure of
derivative interests \parencite{sec2023modernization}. The two-business-day amendment rule
directly moves "post-filing accumulation" \emph{independent of the initial-deadline change}, so
that outcome is dropped as a first-stage margin. We restrict tier-1 outcomes (subsection 3) to
those only the initial-deadline change can move: trigger-to-filing accumulation share, stake at
filing, entry counts and composition, and Item 6 derivative-language rates. As a composition
test for the simultaneous 13G restructuring, we tabulate the switching rate of near-5\%
holders between Schedule 13G and Schedule 13D filings in the quarters surrounding the reform;
a level shift in that rate is evidence the 13G bundling, not the 13D deadline, is doing the
work on any outcome defined over the pooled 13D/13G population.

\subsection{Exposure: From Activist History to a Predetermined Target-Level Measure}

The pivot's original exposure variable was a leave-one-out mean of the activist's own historical
accumulation behavior, with activist fixed effects $\gamma_j$ absorbing the between-activist
variation the measure needs to identify anything. That design is infeasible at the relevant
population. \textcite{polk2024} report 9{,}685 initial Schedule 13D filings by 5{,}863 reporting
entities over 2001--2022, or 1.65 filings per entity. This project's own 300-filing sample tells
the same story directly: 259 unique filer CIKs, 29 filers with two or more filings, and exactly
3 filers with three or more. A leave-one-out mean needs at least two pre-2024 campaigns per
activist, and $\gamma_j$ additionally restricts identification to activists observed both before
and after February 2024 -- on the order of a few dozen funds. External validity then narrows
from "13D filers" to "Sharkwatch-50-type funds," precisely the population for which the
accumulation constraint binds least, since these are the filers best able to pre-position and
substitute into derivatives.

We therefore adopt $\text{RTD}_i$ (subsection 1) as the primary exposure measure and retain
activist-history exposure only as a secondary heterogeneity cut (an indicator for activists
observed in at least two pre-2024 non-corporate-action campaigns), not as the identifying
variable. Because $\text{RTD}_i$ is defined at the target level, it makes
activist$\times$quarter fixed effects estimable: identification runs within an activist across
targets and quarters, which absorbs activist-level shocks that a target-industry$\times$time
fixed effect cannot reach -- fund flows, a manager's strategy shift, or the differential ramp-up
of universal-proxy-card-driven board-contest returns across 2023--2024.

\paragraph{Covariate imbalance and the FE response.} Realized accumulation behavior is not a
clean proxy for the legal constraint: the Commission's own Table 6 shows that non-corporate-action
campaigns under 90\% accumulated by the amended deadline differ sharply from campaigns
that finished on time. Average target market capitalization is \$1.8B versus \$916M; the Amihud
illiquidity ratio is 0.09 versus 0.13 (lower Amihud is higher liquidity, so the campaigns that
did \emph{not} finish by the deadline sit in the \emph{more} liquid, and much larger, targets:
realised slow accumulation marks big campaigns, not illiquid stocks); the
prominent-activist share is 43.6\% versus 29.8\%; the filing-window cumulative abnormal return is
17.2\% versus 5.7\%; and the average increase in shareholder value per campaign is \$222M versus
\$36M \parencite{sec2023modernization}. $\text{RTD}_i$, built from liquidity and size rather than
realized accumulation, is designed to avoid mechanically encoding "large campaign by a large
activist," but it will still correlate with target size and illiquidity by construction. We
therefore saturate every specification with liquidity-decile$\times$quarter and
size-decile$\times$quarter fixed effects in addition to activist$\times$quarter, and report the
covariate balance of targets across $\text{RTD}_i$ bins as a standing diagnostic table before any
point estimate is presented, following the logic of stealth-trading detection-avoidance
constraints on accumulation speed \parencite{collindufresnefos2015,collindufresnefos2016} and
the liquidity-governance tradeoff that motivates using $L$ as a covariate rather than an outcome
\parencite{edmansfangzur2013}.

\subsection{Outcome Hierarchy and the MDE-Honesty Table}

Before any regression is run, we state the population magnitude the design can hope to detect.
By the Commission's own Table 3, the accumulation window binds materially (under 90\% completed
by the amended deadline) on 3\% of non-corporate-action campaigns, about 7 per year; only 1\%
fall under 75\% completed, about 1 per year \parencite{sec2023modernization}. Table 6 row 8 shows
that the 20\% of campaigns that continue accumulating past the deadline acquire, on average,
only 5.9\% of their reported stake inside the window \parencite{sec2023modernization}. Combined,
the population-average treatment intensity of the accumulation channel is on the order of a
tenth of a percentage point of shares outstanding. No result below is credited as more than this
magnitude can support.

Table~\ref{tab:outcome-hierarchy} sets the outcome hierarchy. Tier 1 outcomes are near-term,
extensive-margin or predetermined, and powered at the realistic post-reform sample size (roughly
540--700 non-corporate-action campaigns over 2024--2026, before any repeat-activist
restriction). Tier 2 outcomes require price data and are moderately powered. Tier 3 is reported
descriptively, with confidence intervals, and is never presented as a headline test: the
Post$\,\times\,$RTD$\,\times\,$SaleIntent triple interaction on bid hazard has an ex-ante minimum
detectable effect of 17--31 percentage points on a roughly 17-percentage-point base rate, and the
materially-exposed post-reform cell (the SEC's own under-90\% margin) contains on the order of
three bids over the sample window. A test that cannot distinguish a null from a doubling of the
base rate is not a test.

\subsection{First-Stage Battery: EDGAR-Only, Price-Free, Pre-Registered}

Before any model investment, we run a first-stage battery that requires no price data and is
pre-registered against ex-ante minimum detectable effects computed from the Commission's own
Tables 3, 5, and 6, not from the realized sample. Five components:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Untrimmed delay distributions, split by corporate-action status.} This project's
own fact1 pipeline reports a post-reform (2024Q3--Q4) median delay of 5.0 business days versus
7.0 pre-reform (2023Q2--Q3), and a within-5-business-day share of 75.6\% post versus 35.7\% pre.
The 75.6\% figure depends on an undocumented trim: it drops observations above 60 business days,
and recomputing directly from the full set of 96 non-missing post-period delay observations in
this project's own filing-level data gives a within-5-business-day share of 70.8\% (68 of 96),
not 75.6\%. The pooled sample also mixes corporate-action and non-corporate-action filings,
which the Commission finds have very different late-filing rates (34\% versus 11\%)
\parencite{sec2023modernization}. Both defects are fixed here: we report the untrimmed
compression statistic with the trim as a footnoted robustness check, and we split every delay
statistic by tabular-Item-5(c)-history status once that classifier exists (subsection 7).
\item \textbf{Stake-at-filing distributions}, pre/post, to test the deterrence prediction
directly on a predetermined outcome.
\item \textbf{Entry and composition counts by a coarse, price-free RTD proxy} (e.g. pre-trigger
share turnover deciles), since the full $\text{RTD}_i$ measure requires ADV data whose access is
gated (subsection 7); this proxy lets the first-stage pass run before that gate resolves.
\item \textbf{Item 6 derivative-language rates}, pre/post, as the direct test of substitution
through the derivative-disclosure hole \parencite{sec2023modernization}.
\item \textbf{13G-to-13D switching}, as the composition test described in subsection 1.
\end{enumerate}

The gate rule: filing-delay compression and post-deadline pre-filing accumulation are mechanical
identities under the reform and cannot be used to pass any decision gate, because a merely
compliant world produces both with zero behavioral content.

\subsection{Inference Battery}

With one adoption date, continuous dose, and no staggered-timing structure, the applicable
threat is not negative-weighting in a staggered design but a fragile continuous-dose estimate
that could be an artifact of the specific placebo date, the specific exposure assignment, or the
2023--2025 M\&A and rate cycle. The battery:
\begin{itemize}
\item at least 500 placebo reform dates drawn from 2015--2023, re-estimating the full
specification at each and reporting the realized estimate's percentile in the placebo
distribution;
\item at least 500 permutations of $\text{RTD}_i$ across activists, holding the panel structure
fixed, for the same percentile test;
\item exposure-bin quarterly event studies from 2019 onward, with a joint pre-trend F-test as a
precondition for any causal claim;
\item a non-US activist-block sample (UK DTR5 / EU Transparency Directive notifications) over the
same calendar window, used strictly as a control for the global M\&A and rate cycle and never as
a legal-threshold comparison, since the disclosure regimes are not otherwise comparable.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anatomy of Target Gains (Second Act)}

The second act decomposes total target gains into filing, run-up, and markup components:
$G_{\text{fil}} = \log(P^F/P^0)$, $G_{\text{run}} = \log(P^B/P^F)$, and
$G_{\text{mkp}} = \log(B/P^B)$, where $P^0$ is the unaffected price, $P^F$ the post-filing price,
$P^B$ the bid-eve price, and $B$ the final offer. We fix $P^0$ at a common horizon of 60 trading
days before the trigger date in both the pre- and post-reform cohorts (robustness at 30 and 120
trading days), so that the anchor is not itself shifted by treatment. Because the pre-trigger
toehold phase is untimed, the activist's own buying can reach into the anchor window ---
mechanically so where $\text{RTD}_i > 60$ --- so we diagnose pre-trigger own-trading in the
anchor window from the Item~5(c) sixty-day transaction tables and lean on the 120-day anchor
where contamination is flagged.

The reform compresses the trigger-to-filing interval by roughly two business days in this
project's own data (median delay 7.0 to 5.0 business days), which mechanically shrinks the
calendar span inside $G_{\text{fil}}$ and lengthens the span inside $G_{\text{run}}$ with zero
behavioral content -- and does so in the direction opposite the reform's naive prediction that
earlier disclosure should raise $G_{\text{fil}}$'s share of total gain. Every prediction here is
therefore restated on event-time-invariant objects: the filing-date $[-1,+1]$ cumulative abnormal
return as a share of total gain, with the trigger-to-bid horizon held fixed, rather than on
calendar-spanning segments. Two placebos separate the mechanical artifact from any behavioral
effect. The \emph{mechanical placebo} recuts pre-reform campaigns as if they had filed under a
five-business-day rule, which isolates the pure truncation artifact directly from pre-period
data. The \emph{behavioral placebo} restricts to already-fast filers -- campaigns that filed
within five business days even under the old ten-calendar-day rule -- for whom the reform changes
nothing mechanically, so any measured shift in their gain decomposition is a pure cycle or
composition effect, not a deadline effect.

Where an M\&A outcomes database is unavailable (subsection 7), the acquired-target subsample is
hand-collected from 8-K and DEFM14A filings, following measurement conventions from the M\&A
literature on toeholds, premia, and deal outcomes \parencite{bett2014,eckbomalenkothorburn2025}.
Capitalization -- completion probability up, conditional premium down through weakened target
resistance -- is treated throughout as a measurement lemma inside this anatomy, with the
interpretation credited to \textcite{celentanolevine2025} and the resistance-weakening mechanism
to \textcite{corumlevit2019}, not restated as this paper's own headline finding. Heterogeneity is
explored through the appropriability remnants of the disclosure-timing channel: industry M\&A
intensity as a proxy for fringe-raid probability, strategic-buyer share as a proxy for the
portability of the activist's information rents to a bidder (the broker/intermediary framing of
\textcite{burkartlee2022}), and a regression-discontinuity-style check around the 5\% stake
threshold itself. The classic activist-return decomposition of \textcite{brav2008} anchors the
overall magnitude of gains against which this design's event-time-invariant restatement is
compared.

\subsection{Gates, Cut Order, and Scoop Defense}

\textbf{Gate 0 (week 1): advisor ratification.} \textbf{Gate 1 (weeks 1--2): access.}
CRSP/WRDS and the Bloomberg M\&A checklist are resolved as a hard go/no-go, since every
price-based tier-2/tier-3 outcome and the anatomy of gains depend on one or the other and
neither has been confirmed to date. \textbf{Gate 2 (weeks 2--4): pre-registered EDGAR-only
first-stage pass}, using the battery in subsection 4 and the non-mechanical margin rule stated
there. If at least one non-mechanical margin moves at its pre-registered MDE, the deadline-
accumulation model receives further investment (beyond the frozen provisional lens of
Section~\ref{sec:model}) as the disciplining lens for the headline result; if none moves, the
paper is re-aimed at the accounting lemma, the corner-solution model, and a well-measured policy
null, with the anatomy of gains carrying the paper rather than the chilling result.

\textbf{Published cut order} if the schedule slips: merger chronologies (already excluded) $\to$
the signaling block $\to$ the full deal-universe anatomy (shrinking to the hand-collected
acquired-target subsample) $\to$ the 2010--2021 backfill $\to$ an EDGAR-only fallback carrying
only the first-stage facts and the exposure-composition results.

\textbf{Scoop defense.} The first-stage facts and the exposure design are posted to SSRN once
the exposure measure locks, at approximately month 4--5, ahead of the full anatomy-of-gains
work. The novelty sweep and the publication status of \textcite{celentanolevine2025} are
re-checked at every subsequent revision, since that paper's own capitalization interpretation is
the one this design's measurement lemma must continue to cite rather than re-derive.
