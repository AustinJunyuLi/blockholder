\section{Model: A Deadline Theory of Activist Stake Formation}
\label{sec:model}

This section builds the lens through which the empirical evaluation is read. It is
deliberately small. Its job is to (i) \emph{derive} the exposure variable
$\mathrm{RTD}_i$ that the empirical design uses, rather than assert it; (ii) deliver a
signed prediction for how a shortening of the filing window moves the undisclosed stake
an activist can accumulate before filing; (iii) state the entry response as a testable inequality rather than a
sign; and (iv) supply the exact measurement content of the gain decomposition, so that
the second-act anatomy has a restriction to test and not merely an accounting identity
to report. The capitalization interpretation of a compressed measured premium is
\emph{not} claimed here as a contribution: \textcite{celentanolevine2025} state and
quantify that mechanism, and \textcite{bett2014} own the run-up/markup substitution
evidence. What is new is the time margin. Neither \textcite{celentanolevine2025} (no
disclosure rule) nor \textcite{ordonezcalafibernhardt2022} (a \emph{size} threshold)
models the window as a deadline in an accumulation problem. Target: 10--14 pages in the
paper; results are numbered R1--R6.

\subsection{Environment and timeline}
\label{sec:model-env}

A single risk-neutral activist faces a single target firm $i$ with $S_{\mathrm{out}}$
shares outstanding and liquidity $L_i$, measured empirically by average daily volume
$\mathrm{ADV}_i$ over the six months ending 60 trading days before the trigger ---
lagged so the untimed toehold phase does not mechanically inflate the measure, aligned
with the $P^{0}$ anchor --- (or, equivalently in the cross-section, by
Amihud illiquidity). All agents discount at $\delta \in (0,1)$ per period. Dates:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[$t_0$.] \textbf{Opportunity arrival.} The activist privately draws an intervention
opportunity $\theta \ge 0$: the per-share value improvement available if the campaign
succeeds. The market price at a fixed horizon of $k$ trading days before the trigger
date $t_1$ is $P^{0}$, the reference price. Dating $P^{0}$ at trigger-minus-$k$, not at
filing-minus-$k$, keeps the anchor outside the statutory window, whose accumulation moves
the price; because the pre-trigger toehold phase is untimed, purchases inside
$(t_1-k,\,t_1)$ can still contaminate $P^{0}$ --- mechanically so where
$\mathrm{RTD}_i > k$ --- so the empirical section diagnoses pre-trigger own-trading in the
anchor window from the Item~5(c) sixty-day transaction tables and reports the
120-trading-day anchor as robustness.
\item[$t_1$.] \textbf{Trigger and accumulation window.} The activist first accumulates a
toehold up to the statutory five-per-cent threshold at its own pace --- the pre-trigger
phase carries no deadline. Crossing the threshold is the \emph{trigger date} (the
adopting release's own anchor) and starts the statutory clock: over the following $d$
business days the activist buys the undisclosed increment $x$ (a fraction of shares
outstanding beyond the threshold stake). Pre-reform $d \approx 7$ business days (ten
calendar days); post-reform $d = 5$.
\item[$t_2$.] \textbf{Mandatory filing} at $\min\{$deadline, voluntary filing$\}$. Early
voluntary filing is permitted and is sometimes optimal; the legal deadline is therefore a
constraint that binds on a model-generated subset, not on everyone. The post-filing price
is $P^{F}$ and $G_{\mathrm{fil}} \equiv \log(P^{F}/P^{0})$.
\item[$t_3$.] \textbf{Bidder entry.} Potential acquirers search and evaluate; the bid-eve
price is $P^{B}$ and $G_{\mathrm{run}} \equiv \log(P^{B}/P^{F})$.
\item[$t_4$.] \textbf{Bargaining and resolution.} A final offer $B$ is agreed or the firm
remains freestanding; $G_{\mathrm{mkp}} \equiv \log(B/P^{B})$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{One type dimension.} The activist's type is the scalar $\theta$. We do
\emph{not} carry a second, operational-versus-sale type dimension. This is a deliberate
restriction, and the reason is stated plainly: a two-dimensional type with a
two-dimensional signal $(x,\text{Item 4 purpose})$ generically separates, $P^{F}$
collapses to the full-information price, and the noise the rest of the model needs
disappears. The operational-versus-sale distinction enters this paper only through the
\emph{empirical} Item 4 classification, where it indexes heterogeneity in the entry
elasticities of R4 --- it is not a second signalling dimension. See
\S\ref{sec:model-omitted}.

\subsection{Accumulation technology}
\label{sec:model-cost}

The activist trades against a competitive dealer sector with linear price impact
$\lambda_L(L)$, decreasing in liquidity $L$ (empirically, $\lambda_L \propto 1/\mathrm{ADV}$
or $\propto$ Amihud). Detection risk and the dealer's inventory limit impose a
participation cap: at most a fraction $\phi$ of $\mathrm{ADV}$ may be bought per day.
Buying at daily rate $r$ against linear impact costs $(\lambda_L/2)\,r^{2}$ per day in
excess of the prevailing price; a constant rate $r = x/d$ is optimal under a convex
per-day cost, so the total accumulation cost over the window is

\begin{lemma}[R1: Accumulation cost from a dealer participation cap]
\label{lem:R1}
Under linear dealer price impact $\lambda_L(L)$ and a constant-rate purchase of $x$ over
$d$ days,
\begin{equation}
C(x;d,L) \;=\; \frac{\lambda_L(L)}{2}\,\frac{x^{2}}{d},
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
and therefore, for $x>0$ and $d>0$,
\begin{equation}
C_x = \frac{\lambda_L x}{d} > 0, \qquad
C_{xx} = \frac{\lambda_L}{d} > 0, \qquad
C_{xd} = -\,\frac{\lambda_L x}{d^{2}} < 0,
\label{eq:cost-derivs}
\end{equation}
with the exact horizon scaling $C(x;\kappa d, L) = \kappa^{-1} C(x;d,L)$ for any
$\kappa > 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Derivation sketch]
Per-day excess cost at rate $r$ is $\int_{0}^{r}\lambda_L u\,du = (\lambda_L/2) r^{2}$.
Minimizing $\sum_{t=1}^{d}(\lambda_L/2) r_t^{2}$ subject to $\sum_t r_t = x$ is a convex
problem with symmetric constraint, so $r_t = x/d$; substituting gives
$d\cdot(\lambda_L/2)(x/d)^2 = (\lambda_L/2)x^{2}/d$. The derivatives and the degree
$-1$ homogeneity in $d$ follow by differentiation. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Three consequences matter. First, the cross-partial $C_{xd}<0$ --- the object on which
the paper's only theoretical novelty rests --- is now a \emph{theorem}, not an
assumption. Second, the cost is monotone in liquidity through $\lambda_L(L)$, and the
liquidity object in the model is the same object the regressions use. Third, the horizon
scaling is quantitative: the reform's move from $d\approx 7$ to $d=5$ business days
raises the quadratic component of accumulation cost, at any fixed post-trigger increment,
by the factor $7/5 = 1.4$, i.e.\ by $40\%$. The same mechanism delivers, for free, an
endogenous pre-filing run-up whose magnitude scales with $\lambda_L x/d$ --- a sharper
cross-sectional prediction about the split between leakage and filing CAR than a
reduced-form cost can generate.

\paragraph{What this shortcuts.} Equation~\eqref{eq:cost} is a static reduced form for a
dynamic problem. A full deadline-Kyle treatment in the manner of
\textcite{collindufresnefos2016} would endogenize the intensity path, let the activist
trade off impact against information leakage over the window, and generate a
non-constant rate near the deadline; \textcite{back2018} is the corresponding strategic
benchmark with an endogenous informed trader. What that machinery would add is the
\emph{shape} of the within-window trading path, not the sign of $C_{xd}$ or the $1/d$
scaling, both of which survive as long as per-period impact is convex.
\textcite{collindufresnefos2015} establish that informed trading inside the 13D window is
a real and measurable phenomenon, which is what licenses treating the window as a
binding trading horizon at all; \textcite{kyle1985} supplies the linear-impact primitive.

\subsection{The deadline and who it binds}
\label{sec:model-corner}

The participation cap converts the legal window into a feasibility constraint. Over the
$d$ post-trigger days the activist can add at most $\phi\,\mathrm{ADV}_i\,d$ shares,
i.e.\ an increment
\begin{equation}
\bar{x}_i(d) \;=\; \frac{\phi\,\mathrm{ADV}_i\,d}{S_{\mathrm{out},i}} .
\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
Define the target's \emph{required trading days} per five percentage points of
ownership at the participation cap,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RTD}_i \;\equiv\; \frac{0.05 \times S_{\mathrm{out},i}}{\phi \times \mathrm{ADV}_i},
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
\bar{x}_i(d) \;=\; 0.05\,\frac{d}{\mathrm{RTD}_i}.
\label{eq:rtd}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[R2: Corner-solution heterogeneity and the derived exposure measure]
\label{prop:R2}
Let $x^{u}(d)$ denote the unconstrained optimum of the activist's problem
$\max_{x} \; b(x,d)\,-\,C(x;d,L)\,-\,F$, where $x$ is the post-trigger increment (the
untimed toehold is fixed at the threshold), $b$ is the gross benefit of a stake and
$F$ a fixed campaign cost. Then the activist's increment is
$x^{\ast}_i(d) = \min\{x^{u}(d),\,\bar{x}_i(d)\}$, and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] $\bar{x}_i(d) \ge 0.05 \iff \mathrm{RTD}_i \le d$: the window permits
an undisclosed post-trigger increment of at least another five percentage points --- a
doubling of the threshold stake --- if and only if $\mathrm{RTD}_i \le d$. The maximum
stake acquirable before disclosure, $0.05 + \bar{x}_i(d)$, is decreasing in
$\mathrm{RTD}_i$.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] Under the five-percentage-point benchmark increment, the constrained
set is $\{i : \mathrm{RTD}_i > d\}$ (in general, $\{i : \bar{x}_i(d) < x^{u}_i(d)\}$); it is
increasing (in the set-inclusion sense) as $d$ falls, and the shadow value of the
constraint, $b_x(\bar{x}_i(d),d) - C_x(\bar{x}_i(d);d,L)\ \ge 0$, is increasing in
$\mathrm{RTD}_i$ on that set.
\item[\textup{(iii)}] Hence treatment intensity is a model object: the reform's dose at
target $i$ is a monotone function of $\mathrm{RTD}_i/d$, and the binary treated
indicator is $\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{RTD}_i > d\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Part~(i) is the model's reading of the accumulation ladder tabulated in the adopting
release \parencite{sec2023modernization}: the large majority of filers who complete
accumulation within five business days of the trigger, and the near-universal share who
complete within ten, sit where the window's capacity exceeds their desired increment ---
the deadline is slack for them. That
ladder is therefore not an empirical caveat bolted onto the design; it is the model's
own prediction about which targets the reform can possibly bind on. Part~(iii) is the
point of the whole subsection: the empirical exposure variable is \emph{derived} from
\eqref{eq:cap}--\eqref{eq:rtd}, uses only pre-trigger, target-level, price-free inputs,
and is defined for one-time filers --- which is what makes activist$\times$quarter fixed
effects feasible in the empirical section.

\begin{proposition}[R3: The window comparative static]
\label{prop:R3}
Suppose $b(\cdot,d)$ is $C^{2}$ and the objective is strictly concave in $x$
($b_{xx} - C_{xx} < 0$). Then on the interior (unconstrained) region
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx^{\ast}}{dd} \;=\; -\,\frac{b_{xd} - C_{xd}}{\,b_{xx} - C_{xx}\,}
\;>\;0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
b_{xd} \;>\; C_{xd} \;=\; -\,\frac{\lambda_L x^{\ast}}{d^{2}} .
\label{eq:sc}
\end{equation}
On the constrained region $x^{\ast}_i = \bar{x}_i(d)$ and
$dx^{\ast}_i/dd = \phi\,\mathrm{ADV}_i/S_{\mathrm{out},i} > 0$ unconditionally.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Monotone comparative statics on a concave objective: the sign of $dx^{\ast}/dd$ is the
sign of the cross-partial of the objective, $b_{xd} - C_{xd}$, by the implicit function
theorem applied to the first-order condition, the second-order condition supplying the
negative denominator. The constrained case is immediate from \eqref{eq:cap}. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Honesty condition (SC).} Condition~\eqref{eq:sc} is not automatic and we
flag it as the model's load-bearing single-crossing requirement. The benefit $b$ depends
on $d$ through the entry margin: a longer window means a later filing, hence later
certification and a possibly lower probability that a bid arrives while the activist is
still positioned, so $b_{xd}$ may be negative. The condition states that the marginal
benefit of stake must not fall too fast in $d$ --- specifically, $|b_{xd}| <
\lambda_L x^{\ast}/d^{2}$ whenever $b_{xd}<0$. It is a genuine restriction, it is
weakest exactly where the accumulation friction is largest (high $\lambda_L$, low $d$,
high $x$ --- the constrained targets), and it holds vacuously on the corner. We state it
as a condition rather than assume it away.

\subsection{Bidder entry: certification against deterrence}
\label{sec:model-entry}

Potential acquirers are ex ante identical and free to enter: each pays a search and
evaluation cost $s$ to learn its own valuation, and enters until expected profit is
zero. Two forces run in opposite directions, and the paper's earlier companion model
carried only the second.

\emph{Certification.} A 13D filing is a public, costly-to-fake signal that a large,
sophisticated holder has identified the target and will not defend the incumbent
management, which lowers $s$: the filing does part of the acquirer's screening for it.
This is the solicitation-and-search technology of \textcite{corumlevit2019} and the
activist-as-intermediary/brokerage technology of \textcite{burkartlee2022}; we take both
as given rather than rebuild them.

\emph{Deterrence.} Disclosure raises the price the acquirer expects to pay: the
disclosed stake $x$ and the engagement it implies are capitalized into $P^{F}$, and a
larger, better-informed bloc bargains harder. This is the preemption channel of
\textcite{fishman1988} and the empirical toehold/disclosure-reform finding of
\textcite{bonettiduroormazabal2020}, and it is the force that produced $\partial p /
\partial \pi < 0$ in the author's companion model. That result is nested here, not
renounced: it is the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{det}}$ term below.

Let $h(s, B^{e})$ be the free-entry bid hazard, decreasing in both arguments, and let
$e$ denote the \emph{earliness} of disclosure (minus the filing lag), holding the
campaign fixed.

\begin{proposition}[R4: Certification versus deterrence]
\label{prop:R4}
Define the two nonnegative elasticities
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}} \;\equiv\;
\Bigl(-\tfrac{\partial \log h}{\partial \log s}\Bigr)\Bigl(-\tfrac{\partial \log s}{\partial e}\Bigr),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{det}} \;\equiv\;
\Bigl(-\tfrac{\partial \log h}{\partial \log B^{e}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac{\partial \log B^{e}}{\partial e}\Bigr).
\]
Then $\dfrac{d \log h}{d e} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{det}}$,
so the bid hazard rises after earlier disclosure if and only if
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cert}} > \varepsilon_{\mathrm{det}}$.
\end{proposition}

The sign is therefore an empirical question with named heterogeneity. Certification
should dominate where search is expensive and the counterparty set is not obvious:
opaque or hard-to-value assets, few natural strategic buyers, cold industry M\&A
markets, and Item 4 language expressing sale or strategic-alternatives intent.
Deterrence should dominate where acquirers are already searching --- hot industry M\&A
intensity, an obvious short list of strategic buyers --- so that the certification
elasticity is small while the price effect is not. One complication is worth stating
now: the reform does not move $e$ alone. Through R3 it also lowers $x^{\ast}$, which
lowers $B^{e}$ and therefore \emph{raises} entry, a third term partially offsetting
deterrence. The empirical design must not attribute the composite to certification.

\subsection{Bargaining and appropriability}
\label{sec:model-bargain}

If a bid arrives, the final offer splits the surplus between the acquirer's standalone
valuation $V_a$ and the improved value $Y$ under a successful campaign:
\begin{equation}
B \;=\; V_a \;+\; \beta(x)\,\bigl(Y - V_a\bigr),
\qquad
\beta(x) \;=\; \bar{\beta}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{app}},
\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{app}} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:bargain}
\end{equation}
The discipline on $\beta$ is the point. An unrestricted increasing $\beta(x)$ would
assume away the free-rider structure of \textcite{grossmanhart1980freerider}, which is
exactly the circularity the companion paper's appendix was written to remove. We
therefore import that appendix's \emph{conclusion} and none of its primitives:
$\lambda_{\mathrm{app}} = 1 - q(1-\gamma)\psi$, where $q$ is the probability that a
fringe raider appears at the disagreement node, $\gamma \in [0,1]$ is the portability of
the activist's improvement under a different owner, and $\psi \in [0,1]$ is a pivotality
factor. Appropriability falls with the fringe-raid probability and rises with
portability. Two remarks port directly and become the cross-sectional heterogeneity
table of the empirical section: (a) the primitives are \emph{indexable} --- $q$ by
fringe M\&A intensity in the target's industry-year, $\gamma$ by the
strategic-versus-financial composition of likely acquirers and by asset redeployability,
the charter dilution parameter by state and charter antitakeover provisions, and
pivotality by the bloc's stake against control thresholds; and (b)
$\lambda_{\mathrm{app}}$ \emph{jumps up discontinuously} as the bloc's stake crosses the
blocking threshold $1-\tau_c$, a testable stake-size threshold effect that is directly
about $x$ and therefore directly about the deadline. We do \emph{not} import the tender
game itself: the entry-cost distribution, the synergy distribution, charter dilution and
the control threshold are four new primitives a fourteen-page model cannot afford. (Note
the symbol clash: charter dilution in that appendix was $\phi$; here $\phi$ is the
participation cap. The paper renames the former $\phi_c$.)

\subsection{Measurement lemma}
\label{sec:model-measurement}

Total target gain from the unaffected price to the final offer decomposes exactly:
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{tot}} \;\equiv\; \log\!\frac{B}{P^{0}}
\;=\; \underbrace{\log\!\frac{P^{F}}{P^{0}}}_{G_{\mathrm{fil}}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\log\!\frac{P^{B}}{P^{F}}}_{G_{\mathrm{run}}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\log\!\frac{B}{P^{B}}}_{G_{\mathrm{mkp}}} .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
This is an identity, and we label it as one. Its non-trivial content is the following.

\begin{lemma}[R5: Markup floor and the invariance restriction]
\label{lem:R5}
Let the bid-eve price be the discounted expectation of resolution, $P^{B} = \delta\,[\,
\varrho B + (1-\varrho) P^{\mathrm{fail}}\,]$, where $\varrho \in (0,1]$ is the
completion probability and $P^{\mathrm{fail}} < B$ the freestanding value. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] \textbf{Floor.}
$G_{\mathrm{mkp}} = \log(1/\delta) \;-\; \log\!\bigl(\varrho + (1-\varrho)
P^{\mathrm{fail}}/B\bigr) \;\ge\; \log(1/\delta) > 0$,
i.e.\ in simple returns the measured markup is bounded below by $(1-\delta)/\delta$ plus
a strictly positive deal-risk term. \emph{A fully anticipated deal still shows a positive
markup}; a shrinking measured markup is therefore not, by itself, evidence of anything.
\item[\textup{(b)}] \textbf{Invariance.} Holding the transaction fixed ($B$ and $P^{0}$
unchanged), \eqref{eq:decomp} implies
$dG_{\mathrm{mkp}} = -\,\bigl(dG_{\mathrm{fil}} + dG_{\mathrm{run}}\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Part~(b) is the only thing in the neighbourhood that distinguishes capitalization from
value destruction in data: under pure capitalization the three components reshuffle and
$G_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is invariant; under value destruction $G_{\mathrm{tot}}$ falls.
\textcite{celentanolevine2025} assert the capitalization reading for their structural
counterfactual; \textcite{bett2014} document run-up/markup substitution once run-ups are
value-adjusted --- \textcite{schwert1996} found little substitution without that
adjustment, and the difference is precisely the invariance test. Our lemma states the
exact testable content of the claim, and the empirical section implements it in
\emph{event-time-invariant} form, because the naive calendar-window version carries a
truncation artifact running opposite to its own prediction.

\subsection{The policy sufficient statistic}
\label{sec:model-policy}

\begin{result}[R6: Welfare derivative for target shareholders]
\label{res:R6}
Let $W(d)$ be expected target-shareholder gain. On the interior region the envelope
theorem kills the indirect effect of $d$ through $x^{\ast}$ on the \emph{activist's own}
payoff, so every $dx^{\ast}/dd$ term surviving in $W'(d)$ is a pure externality; on the
constrained set the window's shadow value enters the activist's own payoff first-order,
with the constrained response $dx^{\ast}/dd = \phi\,\mathrm{ADV}_i/S_{\mathrm{out},i}$
itself part of term (iii)'s comparative static. In both regions,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dW}{dd}
= \underbrace{\frac{\partial \Prob(\mathrm{bid})}{\partial d}\;
   \E\bigl[G_{\mathrm{tot}} \mid \text{marginal deal}\bigr]}_{\text{(i) entry margin}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial \Prob(\mathrm{camp})}{\partial d}\;
   \E\bigl[G_{\mathrm{tot}} \mid \text{marginal campaign}\bigr]}_{\text{(ii) campaign-supply margin}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\Prob(\mathrm{bid})\,
   \frac{\partial \E[G_{\mathrm{mkp}}]}{\partial \beta}\,\beta'(x^{\ast})\,
   \frac{dx^{\ast}}{dd}}_{\text{(iii) stake-bargaining margin}} .
\label{eq:welfare}
\end{equation}
\end{result}

Term~(i) has the sign of R4 and is therefore an estimand, not an assumption. Term~(ii)
is nonnegative --- a longer window supports more campaigns --- so it enters the reform's
welfare change ($\Delta d < 0$) with a minus sign: this is the chilling term, and it is
the paper's headline object. Term~(iii) is the channel T2 flagged as omitted and it runs
through R3 and \S\ref{sec:model-bargain}: a shorter window lowers $x^{\ast}$, which
lowers $\beta(x^{\ast})$, which lowers the conditional markup independently of both
other margins.

The measurability claim needs one further step, and it is the step that makes the
formula usable rather than aspirational. Both conditional expectations in
\eqref{eq:welfare} are \emph{marginal}, not average, objects --- marginal deals are by
construction absent from the counterfactual regime, and deterred campaigns are
unobserved. Substituting the average observed premium would be exactly the
LATE-versus-ATE substitution the identification section rejects elsewhere. The resolution
is that the reform's \emph{compliers are the marginal units}: an
intensity design identified off $\mathrm{RTD}_i$ recovers the behaviour of campaigns at
the margin of being deterred. The exposure-based estimands are therefore the objects the
welfare weights require, and the model and the regressions are estimating the same
thing. This also disciplines the accounting: because accumulation moves the price,
part of $G_{\mathrm{fil}}$ is a transfer from selling shareholders to the activist rather
than a gain, and the welfare object in \eqref{eq:welfare} is defined over a fixed
shareholder cohort dated at trigger-minus-$k$, with the transfer term separated
explicitly.

\subsection{What version 1 deliberately omits}
\label{sec:model-omitted}

\paragraph{The signalling block.} $P^{F}$ is treated as a Bayes update against an
\emph{exogenously garbled} filing: nature adds noise to the disclosed
$(x,\text{purpose})$ pair, and misstatement of Item 4 purpose carries an exogenous
securities-liability cost. We do not solve the game in which an informed activist chooses
$x$ as a signal. The reason is worth stating rather than hiding, because a referee will
find it either way. A continuous signal chosen by a privately informed type admits
pooling, partial-pooling and separating equilibria; without a stated refinement and a
uniqueness claim, $P^{F}$ is not a well-defined object and the mapping from the filing
CAR to a model quantity is not identified. Worse, there is a genuine dilemma at the
closure point: if $B$ in \eqref{eq:bargain} is independent of $P^{F}$, the capitalization
proposition is a tautology (the denominator moves and the numerator cannot); if $B$
depends on $P^{F}$ through a market-price floor on offers, then $P^{F} \to B \to P^{F}$
is a fixed point requiring a contraction condition. We choose the first horn
\emph{explicitly}, which is precisely why R5 is labelled a measurement lemma and why
capitalization is not this paper's headline. Endogenizing the filing price is the natural
follow-on paper, not a footnote in this one.

\paragraph{Not modelled, by design.} The operational-versus-sale split as a second type
dimension (\S\ref{sec:model-env}); the tender game underlying $\lambda_{\mathrm{app}}$
(\S\ref{sec:model-bargain}); bidder competition and repeated fringe arrival; the
within-window trading path (\S\ref{sec:model-cost}); and cash-settled derivative
positions, which the empirical Item 6 substitution test treats as an outside option
without pricing it. This last omission is the one we are least comfortable with, since
the substitution margin is a headline empirical result; the model's role there is to
supply the constrained set $\{\mathrm{RTD}_i > d\}$ that predicts \emph{who} substitutes,
not the price at which they do.

\paragraph{Mechanism budget.} The specification above is the neutralized one: a scalar
type, a quadratic accumulation cost, an exogenously garbled filing, a free-entry bidder
cutoff, and a bargaining share restricted by a single appropriability parameter. Ten to
fourteen pages is feasible for \emph{this} model and is not feasible for the version with
a two-dimensional type, an endogenous informative stake, endogenous purpose, endogenous
entry, endogenous $\beta$, and three endogenous prices --- that specification is
eighteen to twenty-two pages plus appendix, which is how the companion draft reached its
current length. We therefore adopt a standing rule: \emph{any mechanism added after the
model is drafted requires deleting another.}
