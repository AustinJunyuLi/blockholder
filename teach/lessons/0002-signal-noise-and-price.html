Lesson 0002

Signal, noise, and inference

How Northstar reads an imperfect signal, and how the market reads an order that may be noise.

Lesson 1 split public histories into flagged and pooled cells. In the pooled cell, the market sees orders but not who placed them. This lesson teaches one skill. Update a belief with a noisy observation instead of taking the observation literally. Northstar applies that rule to its private signal, then the market applies it to the total order.

Do not take a noisy reading literally

Suppose firms like Harbor Tools are worth 100 on average. Northstar receives a private reading of 130. It trusts 60 percent of the reading's distance from the average. The reading is 30 above average, so Northstar moves its estimate by 18. Its estimate is 118, not 130.

The numerical case

Average value 100, private reading 130, trust weight 0.6.

100 + 0.6 × (130 − 100) = 118

Now name the pieces. Let v be Harbor Tools' standalone value. Northstar observes a signal s. The error ε is independent of value, has mean zero, and is normally distributed. The model writes the signal as

s = v + ε

Value is also normally distributed around its mean μv. These assumptions make the trust weight a variance ratio:

β = σv2 / (σv2 + σε2)

Holding value variance fixed, more signal-error variance makes β smaller. Northstar then stays closer to the average. Its conditional estimate is

E[v | s] = μv + β(s − μv)

Now infer who placed the order

Use a one-day teaching case. Compare an engager who buys one unit with a passive holder who submits no informed order. The full model also allows exit. This case isolates one Bayes calculation. A noise trader may sell one unit, do nothing, or buy one unit. Set κ = 0.30. Then no noise has probability 0.70, while a noise buy and a noise sale each have probability 0.15.

The market sees a total order of +1 and starts with a 50 percent chance of engagement. Two explanations fit:

  • With engagement, Northstar buys +1 and noise is zero. Likelihood 0.70. Prior-weighted mass 0.5 × 0.70 = 0.350.
  • Without engagement in this comparison, Northstar submits zero and noise buys +1. Likelihood 0.15. Prior-weighted mass 0.5 × 0.15 = 0.075.

Bayes' rule divides the engagement mass by the total mass that can explain the observation:

0.350 / (0.350 + 0.075) = 0.8235

So the +1 order raises the engagement belief from 0.5 to about 0.824. Now name the order terms. Let q be Northstar's order, z the noise order, and X the total the market observes. The full model calls the noise-order size z̄. This lesson normalizes that size to one.

X = q + z

Pr(z = 0) = 1 − κ,   Pr(z = ±1) = κ/2

More generally, let r be the prior chance of engagement, and let L1 and L0 be the likelihoods of the observed order with and without engagement:

Pr(engagement | order) = rL1 / [rL1 + (1 − r)L0]

The pooled-cell lab

Choose the observed total order, then change κ. The lab recomputes both prior-weighted masses and the posterior.

This lab sets the noise-order size and Northstar's buy to one. The full model uses a general order size and repeats the inference across several days.

Keep the observed order at +1 and raise κ. A noise buy becomes more likely, while zero noise becomes less likely. For this observation, the posterior falls. That is the result the lab establishes.

It does not establish a global sign for price or takeover premia. The full model combines many order histories, and its competitive price is a conditional expectation solved with the control outcome. Pricing gets its own lesson.

What a filing settles

A filing tells the market that engagement happened, so the engagement posterior equals one in the flagged cell. It does not reveal Harbor Tools' exact value. It also does not make every piece of earlier order flow irrelevant.

The later L2 result is stronger. Under fixed cutoff and execution policies, no feedback from observed orders into the trading plan, and the A7-prime condition that the flagged tuple identifies the informed component, the flagged posterior, price, entry probability, and expected premium do not depend on κ. A filing alone is not enough for that theorem. We will prove it after the needed machinery is in place.
