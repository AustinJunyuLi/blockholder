Lesson 0001

The flag splits the world

Trace one purchase from quiet trading to public filing.

The cast

Northstar Fund. A large shareholder in Harbor Tools. Northstar has a private signal about the firm's value. It can hold, sell, or use voice to push for change. Voice can be private. A public campaign is not required for Northstar to be an activist in this economic story.

Harbor Tools. The company whose shares Northstar owns. Harbor Tools is the target of the engagement, not the activist.

Market makers. Traders who set the share price from public information. They see total buying and selling, with Northstar's orders mixed with unrelated noise trades. They infer. They do not observe Northstar's signal.

A bidder. A possible buyer of Harbor Tools. The bidder enters only when its expected gain covers the share price, entry cost, and takeover premium.

Northstar crosses the line

Start with Northstar at 4.9 percent. It buys enough shares to reach 6 percent and plans to press Harbor Tools privately for a sale. That private pressure is voice. Northstar is now the activist, even if no letter or campaign is public.

US beneficial ownership is not just the name printed on a share certificate. It turns on direct or indirect voting power or investment power, including power to dispose of the shares. Once a person acquires beneficial ownership of more than 5 percent of a covered class, a reporting question arises.

More than 5 percent does not automatically mean Schedule 13D. Eligible institutional, passive, and exempt holders may use Schedule 13G. Schedule 13D gives the market richer information about purpose and plans. In this running example, Northstar has an activist purpose, so the lesson follows the Schedule 13D route. Real eligibility depends on the facts and the applicable rule.

The legal clock and the model clock

Under the current US rule, an initial Schedule 13D is due no later than five business days after the trade date that creates the obligation. A filer may report earlier. Before February 5, 2024, the outer deadline was ten calendar days.

The model makes the clock exact. It calls the threshold τ, the crossing day c, and the filing window T. The filing lands on day c + T. This exact date is a modeling assumption, not a literal claim that real filers always wait until the deadline. The model also treats reaching τ as a crossing, while the US legal trigger is beneficial ownership of more than 5 percent.

Why Northstar buys first

Suppose Northstar still wants more Harbor Tools shares. Before filing, its purchases are mixed with noise trades. The market may notice buying pressure, but it cannot directly identify Northstar's purpose. A purpose-revealing filing can raise the price before Northstar finishes buying. Quiet accumulation lets Northstar buy more of the desired stake before paying that disclosure-induced increase.

This is a preference, not a guarantee. Order flow can move the price before filing, and a filing need not raise the price in every state. The point is that revealing a value-improving plan can make the remaining shares more expensive.

The partition at the decision day

Let H be the day the bidder decides. The model puts every public history into one of two cells.

Flagged. Northstar chose voice, crossed the model threshold, and the filing reached the market by H. The model compresses the Schedule 13D purpose narrative into a truthful engagement flag. The public also sees the reported stake.

Pooled. No purpose-revealing filing reached the market by H. The market must infer from order flow. Pooled does not mean passive. This cell can contain hold, exit, quiet voice below the threshold, or voice whose filing has not yet arrived.

The model flag arrives when voice, crossing, and the clock line up, so c + T ≤ H. At a fixed trading plan, a lower threshold or shorter exact window can move a history from pooled to flagged, not the reverse. This is the clock mapping in result D1.

A filing reveals engagement in this stylized model. It does not by itself prove every other fact the market cares about. A later result needs the reported stake and remaining order to identify the informed component, plus fixed policies and no feedback from observed prices into the trading path. Lesson 1 only needs the two-cell map.

A higher premium can deter a takeover

Assume Harbor Tools is worth $2 billion. The takeover premium is $100 million without engagement and $180 million with engagement. Engagement raises the premium paid to shareholders by $80 million if a bid occurs. Northstar's 6 percent stake receives $4.8 million of that conditional increase.

The bidder faces the other side of the same arithmetic. It must fund the extra $80 million. A bidder with modest synergy may stay out. The model therefore assumes a higher premium with engagement, but it does not assume that engagement makes entry certain. A higher conditional premium can reduce the chance of a bid.

Quick check

Use these questions to catch a mix-up. A perfect score is not evidence of mastery.

1. What makes Northstar the activist in this story?

Voice belongs to the shareholder trying to change the firm. It can happen in private.

2. What can six percent beneficial ownership of a covered class imply?

More than 5 percent does not select the form by itself. Eligibility and purpose matter.

3. How do the two clocks differ?

Real filers can report early. The model imposes the exact date c + T.

4. What can the pooled cell contain?

Pooled describes public information at H. It does not prove that no activism exists.

5. What follows from a higher engaged premium?

Shareholders gain more conditional on a bid. The bidder also pays more, so entry can become less likely.
