# Course plan: from the story to the presentation

This is the route for learning the project from zero and becoming able to present
it. Each numbered item is a module, not a promise of one HTML file. Split a module
into short sessions when it contains more than one learning win. Each session
should end with one thing the learner can say, calculate, or draw without notes.
The order follows the model's dependencies, not the repository layout.

Use sparse cold recall. After 48 to 72 hours, or after every two or three modules,
open with one mixed prompt drawn only from demonstrated learning records. Do not
turn each fact into a separate quiz.

## Part I: learn the world

1. **The cast and the disclosure clock.** Firms, shareholders, blockholders,
   exit, voice, activism, takeovers, 13D, 13G, beneficial ownership, threshold,
   window, and the flagged/pooled split.
2. **Private information and noisy orders.** The signal equation, Gaussian
   projection, ternary noise, Bayes' rule, and the pooled-cell posterior.
3. **How a market maker prices a mixed order.** Conditional expectation, order
   flow, price impact, bidder entry, and why the paper defines liquidity as noise
   intensity rather than depth or turnover.
4. **The two-round timeline.** Complete contingent plans, pooled trading, the
   filing node, flagged trading, the no-feedback assumption, and the clock
   equivalence.

Checkpoint after Part I: give the five-minute story without equations, receive
specific feedback, then retest it after a delay.

## Part II: derive the object and the fixed-policy results

5. **Payoffs and the inner price problem.** Shareholder payoff branches, the
   engagement improvement, premium wedge, bidder-entry rule, and the scalar price
   fixed point. Derive why m0 >= 0 gives uniqueness.
6. **D1 and L1.** Measurability, the two-cell partition, total expectation, null
   cells, and the run-up plus filing-day jump identity.
7. **L2.** The flagged tuple, A7', conditional independence, and the exact reason
   the flagged premium does not depend on kappa at fixed policies.
8. **L3.** The pooled posterior representation under A(tau), the second-difference
   identity, the role of Ch, and the small-posterior expansion.
9. **L4 and T1.** The one-way door, weight versus composition, L4 under threshold
   tightening, T1's threshold and window comparisons, and the W times C iff
   condition for the window margin.

Checkpoint after Part II: draw the partition and derive the premium decomposition
and sensitivity factorisation from memory. Repair errors, then retest after a
delay.

## Part III: equilibrium and evidence

10. **Cutoffs and P1.** Ordered plans, single crossing, best responses, cutoff
    equilibria, Brouwer, A6, A7-J, off-path beliefs, and why existence is not
    uniqueness.
11. **C1.** Fixed-policy attenuation versus general-equilibrium feedback,
    contraction, the dominance margin, the remainder bound, and what 18 of 80
    numerical nodes do and do not establish.
12. **The evidence ledger.** How to read PROVED, NUMERICAL, ESTIMATED, and OPEN;
    the A(tau) support failure, A6 jumps, unresolved grid nodes, and the frozen
    v4 record.
13. **The empirical bridge.** The 2024 13D timing change, filing-delay descriptives,
    run-up/jump timing split, bindingness dose, bounded null, and why the causal
    headline is still pending.

## Part IV: become presentation-ready

14. **The five-minute story retest.** Repeat the Part I checkpoint with no notes
    and repair only the weak transitions.
15. **The whiteboard derivation retest.** Repeat the Part II checkpoint from
    memory and defend each assumption used.
16. **The assumptions defense.** Practice questions about A7', A(tau), A(br),
    fixed policies, m0 >= 0, equilibrium feedback, and the dropped claims.
17. **The full rehearsal.** Run the 15-minute talk and the 40-minute talk as
    separate sessions. Give feedback after each, require a delayed retest, then
    prepare slide transitions, likely objections, and a one-page speaking sheet.

The grand-tour report is an optional map. The modules are the teaching route. The
model note and model card are the authorities when a shortcut needs checking.
