# Mission: present the v4 two-round disclosure model from first principles

## Why

The user wants to understand and present the whole project, starting with no
university-level finance or economics assumed. The course should begin with the
story of firms, blockholders, activism, takeovers, trading, and disclosure. It
should then derive the model carefully enough that the user can reproduce the
equations on a whiteboard, explain every hypothesis, and distinguish a theorem
from a numerical check or an empirical estimate.

## Success looks like

- Explain the institutional and financial setting to a non-specialist before
  introducing any notation.
- Derive the signal, posterior, price, bidder-entry, premium, and sensitivity
  equations line by line, using small numerical examples before the general form.
- State D1, L1, L2, L3, L4, P1, T1, and C1 precisely, give the proof route,
  and identify what fails when a load-bearing hypothesis is removed.
- Reproduce the project's evidence boundary: what the numerical checks establish,
  what the filing sample shows, what remains open, and what v4 deliberately drops.
- Give a coherent 15-minute and 40-minute presentation of the project, including
  likely questions and honest answers.

## Constraints

- Lessons remain short and completable in one sitting, but the sequence is longer.
  Each lesson has one mathematical or presentation win.
- Depth is rigorous derivation plus proof route. Full proof files remain available
  for reference, and difficult steps get their own lesson rather than being waved
  away.
- Footing is zero: Kyle-style market microstructure basics get taught, not assumed.
- All prose follows the unslop rules: no em dashes, sentence-case headings, plain
  words, straight quotes.

## Out of scope

- The retired welfare and planner apparatus, which v4 dropped.
- Rebuilding the full software pipeline as a programming course. We will still
  read its outputs and explain what each check establishes.
