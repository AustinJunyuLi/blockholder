Glossary: the v4 vocabulary

The short definitions used across the course. Print this page or keep it open while you work.

The corporate-finance problem

Agency problem
Managers or controlling insiders may choose actions that do not maximise outside shareholders' value. A blockholder may respond through exit, private engagement, or public pressure.
Free-rider problem
In a tender offer, each shareholder can refuse a price below the expected post-takeover value and still benefit if others tender. The bidder may then be unable to capture enough of the gain to enter.

The rule

Disclosure rule
In the model, a stake threshold and filing window create a public flag that reveals the stake and engagement. The real US system generally requires a holder above 5 percent of a covered class to report on Schedule 13D or, when eligible, Schedule 13G. The forms do not reveal the same information.
Beneficial ownership
Ownership attributed under SEC rules through direct or indirect voting or investment power. It can differ from the shares registered in one account and can include holdings attributed to a group.
Schedule 13D
The fuller US beneficial-ownership report generally required after acquiring more than 5 percent of a covered class unless an exception or a Schedule 13G route applies. It includes purpose and plans. The initial filing is due no later than five business days after the triggering acquisition and may be filed earlier.
Schedule 13G
A shorter US beneficial-ownership report available only through specified eligibility routes. A holder cannot choose it merely because it prefers less disclosure. Filer status, purpose, circumstances, and the applicable deadline all matter.
Threshold margin, τ
The part of the model rule fixing which stake sizes trigger the flag. US reporting begins above 5 percent of a covered class; the model writes the boundary as τ. Lower τ means a tighter threshold.
Window margin, T
The model's exact delay from crossing to filing. Real Schedule 13D law instead sets an outer deadline and permits earlier filing. The current outer deadline is five business days; before 5 February 2024 it was ten calendar days. Lower T means a tighter window.
Stake at filing, BF
The percent of the class of shares printed on the filing itself.
Clock equivalence
For a Voice plan in the model, the flag has landed by decision day H exactly when the stake at H − T has reached τ. This is a model identity, not a rule for calculating a real filing deadline.

The model

Blockholder
The single large shareholder with a private signal who trades and may engage. Never: activist (unless voice is chosen), investor, fund.
Liquidity, κ
Noise-trading intensity in [0, 1]. The paper's driving variable. Never: depth, volume, turnover.
Order flow, X
The total order observed by the market. In the model it combines the blockholder's coarsened order with a noise order, so the market does not see either component by itself.
Market microstructure
The study of how trading rules, orders, and private information shape prices. Here, competitive market makers set price to expected shareholder value using public order flow and any filing.
Posterior, π
The market's probability that engagement is present after using its public information. It equals 1 in the flagged cell and can take any value in [0, 1] in the pooled cell.
Partition
The split of what the market observes into a flagged state (disclosure occurred) and a pooled state (it has not). The paper's identity. Never: signal, information structure.
Flagged cell, 𝒟 = 1
Model histories where the filing landed before the control decision. The filing reveals engagement, so π = 1.
Pooled cell, 𝒟 = 0
Model histories with no filing by the control decision. The market uses order flow to infer engagement. The posterior can lie anywhere in [0, 1], because no filing does not guarantee ambiguity.
Ω
The unconditional probability of the flagged cell. The cell weight in every decomposition.
Premium wedge, Δm
The gap m1 − m0 between the takeover premium with and without engagement. A higher conditional premium can make bidder entry less likely, so it does not guarantee a takeover.
Control outcome
The bidder's entry decision and the resulting shareholder payoff. Engagement can raise non-takeover value and the premium conditional on a bid, while that higher premium can deter entry.
Exit / Hold / Quiet voice / Public voice
The earlier draft's four actions: sell, stay passive, engage below the threshold, or buy above it and become flagged. In v4 they are one special case of the ordered plan menu.
Complete contingent plan
A full stake path and engagement choice for the relevant model contingencies. The path does not change in response to realised order flow or prices during the filing window.
Cutoff rule
A strategy that assigns ordered signal regions to ordered plans. Types below a cutoff choose a less aggressive plan; types above it choose a more aggressive one. Weakly ordered cutoffs allow a plan's region to collapse.

The price path

Run-up, R
The price change from just before the threshold crossing to just before the filing. It can be positive, zero, or negative.
Filing-day jump, J
The price change from the last not-yet-disclosed price to the flagged price. It is also unsigned. The identity PF − PPc− = R + J holds exactly.
Timing split
The division of the target's price reaction into run-up and jump. The headline empirical test.

The results' machinery

Weight effect, W
At fixed plans and cutoffs, tightening either margin moves histories one way from pooled to flagged, so the pooled weight 1 − Ω weakly falls. In the fixed-policy factorisation, this mechanical change attenuates total sensitivity only when the pool's own sensitivity is held fixed. It does not sign the full change by itself.
Composition effect, C
What changes because different histories remain pooled. For threshold tightening, L4 weakly lowers pooled sensitivity only under its bridge assumptions. For window tightening, the composition effect is unsigned. If plans and cutoffs adjust, the general-equilibrium sign also needs C1's dominance-and-contraction conditions.
Honesty label
The tag every result carries: PROVED, NUMERICAL, ESTIMATED, or CONJECTURE. Labels are never weakened by editing.
Feb-2024 acceleration
SEC Release 33-11253 shortened the initial Schedule 13D outer deadline from ten calendar days to five business days, effective 5 February 2024. The course uses this dated rule change as its empirical anchor. Never: the reform, the shock.
