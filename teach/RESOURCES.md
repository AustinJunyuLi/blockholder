### Disclosure and filings

- [SEC final rule: Modernization of Beneficial Ownership Reporting](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2023/10/33-11180)
  The rule page for Release 33-11253. Use for the five-business-day initial
  Schedule 13D deadline, its 2024 effective date, and revised 13G deadlines.
- [SEC Regulation 13D-G staff interpretations](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/corporation-finance-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting)
  Current staff guidance on beneficial ownership and Schedule 13D or 13G filing
  questions. Check it before stating a legal edge case.
- [SEC guide: Using EDGAR to Research Investments](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/using-edgar-research-investments)
  The SEC's plain guide to finding Schedule 13D and 13G filings. Use for filing
  practice and form labels, not for the model's stylized timing.

### Economic foundations

- [Jensen and Meckling (1976), "Theory of the Firm"](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2876%2990026-X)
  The agency-cost foundation. Use when the course separates shareholders' goals
  from managers' incentives.
- [Hirschman (1970), _Exit, Voice, and Loyalty_](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674276604)
  The source of the exit and voice vocabulary. Use before translating those
  choices into blockholder trading and engagement.
- [Grossman and Hart (1980), "Takeover Bids, the Free-Rider Problem, and the Theory of the Corporation"](https://doi.org/10.2307/3003400)
  The tender-offer free-rider problem. Use for why a bidder may not capture all
  value it creates.
- [Kyle (1985), "Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading"](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913210)
  The benchmark for an informed trader hiding in noise. Use for order-flow
  inference, not as a claim that the v4 market is the full Kyle model.
- [Maug (1998), "Large Shareholders as Monitors"](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.35053)
  A model in which liquidity can help a monitor build a stake. Use for the voice
  side of the liquidity debate.
- [Edmans (2009), "Blockholder Trading, Market Efficiency, and Managerial Myopia"](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01508.x)
  A model of governance through informed exit. Use for the price-discipline
  channel.
- [Brav, Jiang, Partnoy, and Thomas (2008), "Hedge Fund Activism, Corporate Governance, and Firm Performance"](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x)
  Primary evidence on activist objectives, tactics, and outcomes. Use for the
  empirical background, while keeping association separate from causation.
- [Coffee (1991), "Liquidity Versus Control"](https://scholarship.law.columbia.edu/faculty_scholarship/28/) and [Bhide (1993), "The Hidden Costs of Stock Market Liquidity"](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2893%2990039-E)
  The exit side of the liquidity debate used in the report. Treat the two papers
  as arguments to compare with Maug, not as a universal sign result.
- [Greenwood and Schor (2009), "Investor Activism and Takeovers"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.05.005)
  Evidence linking activist returns and later takeovers. Use for association,
  not a claim that activism guarantees a bid.
- [Bond, Edmans, and Goldstein (2012), "The Real Effects of Financial Markets"](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-financial-110311-101826) and [Edmans, Goldstein, and Jiang (2012), "The Impact of Prices on Takeovers"](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01738.x)
  Sources for price feedback into real decisions and takeover activity. Use when
  separating price inference from a bidder's response.
