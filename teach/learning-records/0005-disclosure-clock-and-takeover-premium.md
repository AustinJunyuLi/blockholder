# 0005: disclosure clock and takeover premium

Austin connected the reporting threshold, the public-information split, and the
bidder's takeover decision in the Northstar Fund and Harbor Tools example.

## Demonstrated

- Northstar is the activist because it tries to influence Harbor Tools. A public
  campaign is not required.
- Crossing 5 percent starts the reporting clock. Purpose separates an eligible
  passive 13G route from Northstar's activist 13D route.
- The threshold-crossing trade starts the clock. Austin recalled the change from
  the former ten-day deadline to the current five-day deadline.
- A history is pooled when the purpose-revealing filing has not reached the
  market by the bidder's decision. Quiet voice can therefore remain pooled. The
  history becomes flagged when the filing arrives in time.
- The flag reveals Northstar's intention and reported stake. Harbor Tools' true
  value and the bidder's private synergy remain hidden, so neither a takeover nor
  a positive filing-day price jump is guaranteed.
- Northstar prefers to own shares before the flag arrives. Existing shares gain
  if the price rises, while later purchases may cost more.
- A bidder with insufficient synergy can stay out when the takeover premium is
  high. A larger premium conditional on a bid does not guarantee entry.
