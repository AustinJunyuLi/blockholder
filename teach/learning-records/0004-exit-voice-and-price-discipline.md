# 0004: exit, voice, and price discipline

Austin explained both choices with Northstar Fund and Harbor Tools, then repaired
the price channel in his own words.

## Demonstrated

- Exit means Northstar sells its block instead of paying for a campaign. At a
  $2 billion market value, its 6 percent stake is quoted at $120 million. If
  Northstar believes Harbor Tools is worth $1.8 billion, the stake is worth
  $108 million. Selling before the price adjusts avoids a $12 million loss.
- Voice means Northstar keeps the stake and pays the $4 million campaign cost.
  If the campaign adds $100 million, Northstar receives $6 million through its
  6 percent stake and nets $2 million. Doubt about the campaign's effect can make
  exit more attractive.
- A market maker sees selling pressure and infers that Harbor Tools may be worth
  less than $2 billion. The resulting price decline gives management a stronger
  incentive to improve, even without a letter from Northstar.

## Precision retained

The market maker infers possible bad information. The market maker does not know
the firm's value or lower the price merely because the price is already falling.
A lower price strengthens management's incentive to act. It does not guarantee
an improvement.
