## 1. Question

Can corporate insiders — officers and directors — work out that an activist is accumulating a stake **before** the Schedule 13D filing makes it public, and do they trade on that inference? The authors' answer is a new category of informed trading: the insider's edge is *not* inside information about the activist (activist plans are created by an outsider and are not the firm's property, p. 7), but private knowledge of the firm's own fundamentals, which lets her subtract fundamentals-driven order flow from the aggregate tape and attribute the residual to an activist. Because such a trade is not motivated by material nonpublic information about the issuer, it "does not violate any insider trading rule" (p. 4). The economic stake is control defence: insiders who stop selling keep votes at "the dawn of an activist campaign" (p. 23), and they also ride the 13D announcement pop.

## 2. Model / data and method

**Theory (§3, pp. 10–19, proofs in OA Appendices A–B, pp. 2–13).** Three dates $t = 0,1,2$; four agents: activist $A$, insider $I$, stock picker/speculator $N$, market maker $M$ (p. 11). The firm pays $D_2 \in \{D_H > 0, D_L = 0\}$; the activist raises output by $\psi\nu\theta_A$ where $\nu \in \{0,1\}$ is her ability and $\theta_A$ her stake, so **value creation is proportional to the activist's own stake** — there is no acquirer, no bid, no premium. Joint law (eq. 1, p. 12): $\Pr(\nu=1,D_H)=\eta_1$, $\Pr(\nu=0,D_H)=0$, $\Pr(\nu=1,D_L)=\eta_2$, $\Pr(\nu=0,D_L)=\eta_3$ — the activist can always improve a good firm, only sometimes a bad one. Information is nested, $\mathcal F_M \subset \mathcal F_I \subset \mathcal F_A$ (p. 12): activist and insider both know $D_2$; only the activist knows $\nu$; the market maker knows neither. Strategies are binary for tractability: $\theta_A \in \{0,\bar\theta\}$ (buy or abstain), $\theta_I \in \{-\bar\theta, 0\}$ (sell or stay put) (p. 13). The stock picker is the noise-plus-signal trader, $\theta_{N,t} \in \{-\bar\theta,0,\bar\theta\}$, with type-conditional probabilities $\pi^s_k$, $\tilde\pi^s_k$ (eq. 2, p. 14) ordered so buys are likelier in the good state. **Trading is sequential**: the market maker prices $p_1(\theta_A + \theta_{N,0})$ at $t=0$ and $p_2(\theta_I + \theta_{N,1}, \theta_A + \theta_{N,0})$ at $t=1$, both as conditional expectations (eqs. 3–4, p. 14); "both trading dates may fall within the same calendar day" (p. 14). The insider's objective (eq. 6, p. 15) adds a disutility $-\phi\theta_A(-\theta_I)$ from activism, increasing in the activist's stake and in how much she sells. Equilibrium notion: conjecture-and-verify on binary strategies, with a risk-neutral market maker setting each price to the conditional expectation of firm value given the order flows he has seen (eqs. 3–4, p. 14); the first stage is solved in OA Appendix A, the second in Proposition 1.

**Empirics (§§4–5, pp. 19–38; Tables 1–10, pp. 43–53; Tables C1–C7, OA pp. 17–23).** Universe: SEC EDGAR **Schedule 13D filings, 1996–2018**, following Collin-Dufresne–Fos (2015); insider filings, non-market acquisitions and derivative-driven crossings are dropped, and the filer must cross 5% by open-market purchase — ~3,100 preliminary, **2,847 events** with disclosed trade-level data, **115,841 event-days** (2,847 × trading days in the 60-calendar-day window) (pp. 19–20). Merged with CRSP/Compustat and Thomson Reuters Form 4 insider trades for named officer and director role codes; the master panel is **31,899,356 firm-trading-day** observations (Table 2, p. 44). Design: two-way and firm × year-month fixed-effects OLS linear-probability regressions of daily insider buy / sell / net-sell indicators on (i) a 60-day-pre-filing window dummy (eq. 8) and (ii) a same-day "SC13D trade" dummy inside the window (eq. 9), plus a ±5-day lead-lag specification in both directions (eqs. 10–11) to establish who leads. Standard errors clustered by firm throughout. **No instrument, no rule change, no control group of untargeted firms in the within-window tests** — identification rests on saturated fixed effects plus the lead-lag asymmetry.

## 4. Institutional facts used

- **Schedule 13D = >5% beneficial ownership; "filing is mandated within ten days after an investor crosses that threshold"** (fn. 8, p. 7). The **ten-day** rule is the only version in the file; the 2024-02-05 five-business-day amendment is **never mentioned** ("five business" = 0 hits in 79 pages).
- **The 60-day retrospective trading disclosure**: 13D filers must disclose all their transactions in the previous 60 days (fn. 5, p. 5; §4.1, p. 20). This is the paper's whole empirical instrument — it is what makes activist trades observable ex post.
- **Form 4**: insider trades must be disclosed within 48 hours (fn. 6, p. 5; p. 28) — the basis for the reverse-direction T+2 test.
- **T+3 settlement** until 2017: an ownership record change completes three days after the trade (p. 5, pp. 27–28) — the basis for the day +3 result.
- **Rule 10b5-1 plans** and the safe harbour that lets insiders *cancel* precommitted sales, so "informed non-trading" is legal (p. 8; fn. 18, p. 22).
- **Blackout windows** are not disclosed, so they are proxied from Bettis, Coles and Lemmon (2000): free trade = [t+4, t+14], not-free = [t−14, t+2] around the earnings date (p. 22).
- **Market-surveillance vendors as of 2024**: Nasdaq IR Intelligence (with an "activism unit" and "surveillance analysts"), S&P Global, Diligent Market Intelligence, FIS, Q4 (fn. 11, p. 10).
- **Activist trading footprint**: filers trade on 29.2% of window days, taking 26.3% of daily turnover in the 60-day window and **30.1% in the last ten days**; daily turnover 0.63% overall vs 1.23% on filer-trade days; 13D turnover 0.00% vs 0.23% (pp. 9, 20; Table 2, p. 44).
- Sample: 1996–2018; 64 events in 2004 to 185 in 2007, mean 124/yr (p. 20; Figure C2, OA p. 15).

## 5. Referee-facing strengths / weaknesses

**Strengths.**
- The lead-lag design (Tables 7A/7B) is the paper's real identification: insider buying loads on day 0 and T+3 and nothing else, while activist trading does **not** load on T+2 after insider trades. That asymmetry is hard to generate by a common-factor story and it is the reason the paper is publishable without a shock.
- The mechanism test (Table 9) is genuinely discriminating: the response exists only when positive earnings news is *absent*, which is what filtering predicts and what leakage does not.
- Three separate placebo-ish cuts point the same way — hostile campaigns (C5), low-vulnerability firms (C6), true insiders vs other legal insiders (C7).
- Sample size and fixed-effect saturation (firm × year-month) are beyond reproach; the tobit intensive-margin check (C1) matches the extensive margin.
- The theory is honest about its own limits: existence is stated **iff** (B21), the fully revealing states are flagged as an artefact of binary strategies (OA p. 12), and Lemma A1 bounds how often that artefact bites.

**Weaknesses / open flanks.**
- **The headline "insiders buy" does not survive the window-level regression.** Table 4 shows the 60-day-window buy coefficient insignificant (0.0006 [0.0005]); the buying result lives only at daily concurrence (Table 5). The paper concedes this in prose (p. 23: the change "is driven by the slowing of insider selling by 0.91 percentage points"). A referee could say the window-level fact is *less selling*, and the buying fact is a same-day co-movement.
- **The theory is a mechanism illustration, not a source of the estimates.** No proposition is taken to the data as a magnitude; the existence region (R5) is one parameterisation; nothing is calibrated. The model's own comparative statics in $\psi$, $\phi$, $\eta$ are never mapped to Tables 3–10.
- **Fully revealing cells** ($x = -\bar\theta$ and $x = 2\bar\theta$) are an artefact of two-point strategies, admitted at OA p. 12; the insider's indifference at $x = -\bar\theta$ is broken by an *unmodelled* diversification motive (OA p. 11).
- **R² is tiny in the CAR regression** (0.002–0.021, Table 10) and the announcement-return channel is a cross-sectional association with no exogenous variation.
- **No control group and no rule change anywhere.** Every within-window test compares days inside one event to other days inside the same event; the 60-day-window tests compare targeted-firm days to all other firm-days.
- **Internal-consistency slips in the AAM** (see version-risk box): the p. 36 sign on the `Shortfall in insider sell` coefficient, the "basis points" units on p. 9, and the p. 5 / p. 22 / Table 3 disagreement on 13 bp / 15% / 17%.
- **The activists' counter-strategy is documented but not modelled** (R17): activists shift trading into insiders' blackout days. That is a strategic response the three-date model has no room for.

## 6a. THE DECISIVE QUESTION — does this paper refute the partition whitespace?

**One sentence, sourced:** **No** — the paper contains no disclosure rule inside its model at all ("We consider an economy with three dates, t = 0, 1, 2, and four types of investors", p. 11: no filing node, no 5% trigger and no filing window anywhere in the economy, and printed pp. 11–18 — the entire model, equilibrium and Proposition 1 — contain not one occurrence of "disclosure", "13D" or "Schedule"), and it has no bidder, tender offer or takeover premium in 79 pages ("bidder", "tender", "merger", "entry" = 0 hits; "takeover" = 1 hit, on p. 3, as a foil), so it neither partitions control-node histories into flagged and pooled cells by a legal threshold and deadline nor derives any premium or entry comparative statics from such a partition.

**The nearest thing in the paper, stated honestly, because a referee will find it.** The model *does* partition market histories into cells and price each one. Aggregate date-0 order flow takes four values $\theta_A + \theta_{N,0} \in \{-\bar\theta, 0, \bar\theta, 2\bar\theta\}$; two of them are **fully revealing** and two are **pooled** (OA pp. 3 and 11–12), and equilibrium prices are given cell by cell in eqs. (A2) and (B7). Three things keep this outside W3:

1. **The partition is generated by order-flow arithmetic, not by a rule.** It exists because strategies are two-point, so some sums are only achievable one way. It is Kyle–Vila-style revelation, and the authors themselves say it is fragile: "we acknowledge that the fully revealing states are unlikely to arise in a more general setting with a full range of trading strategies" (OA p. 12), with Lemma A1 showing the revealing cell is the *least* likely. The v4 partition is the opposite: legally keyed by $(\tau, T)$ and present in every parameter configuration.
2. **There is no legal timing dimension.** The two model dates "may fall within the same calendar day" (p. 14). A filing window $T$ has nothing to attach to; the window margin cannot even be posed.
3. **The objects are different.** The paper's outcome variables are the insider's trade and the second-stage price. There is no control node, no entrant, and value creation is $\psi\nu\theta_A$ — proportional to the *activist's own stake*, not a premium paid by a third party. $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}(\kappa,\tau,T)$ has no counterpart.

**Verdict for the position file:** the standing risk flagged in `research/positions/ADVERSARIAL_P2.md` (row 6) and in the competitor map's sweep (d) can now be **retired as a refuter and re-entered as an adjacent competitor** on the pooled-cell information structure — with the four overlap risks in §7 written into the draft rather than waved away.

## 7. Implications for our position

**Coordinates they occupy.** Object: the *insider's* trade and the 13D announcement return. Margin of the disclosure rule: **none as a treatment** — the 5% threshold is a sample filter, the ten-day deadline is a subperiod label, and the 60-day retrospective disclosure is a data-generating device. Identification: saturated-FE panel plus a lead-lag asymmetry; no shock, no control group, no instrument. On the three axes that define our position (liquidity $\kappa$ × disclosure-rule margin × control outcome) they occupy **none**.

**Four real overlap risks — these are what the draft must handle.**

1. **They own the claim that *a third party infers activism from pooled order flow, before the flag lands*.** Our pooled cell $\mathcal C_P$ is exactly a market that has not seen the filing and must infer $\pi(\mathcal I) = \Pr(a=1 \mid \mathcal I)$ from the tape. If our contribution ever reads as *someone learns about activism from pooled order flow*, they have it first and in a refereed journal. Our claim must stay on the *legal keying* of the partition and on $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}$, never on the inference itself. Their twist is a **fourth agent** with strictly finer information than the market maker ($\mathcal F_M \subset \mathcal F_I \subset \mathcal F_A$, p. 12) — an agent our two-round model does not have, which is a difference in our favour and a modelling suggestion against us.
2. **They already split the pre-filing period at the deadline, and got a result.** Table 6 (p. 48) runs [−60,−11] against [−10,−1] and finds the effect stronger in the last ten days. That is not a window-margin *change*, but it is the nearest published cut to our run-up split, and it comes with the institutional claim that activist trades "do not incur significant price impacts before reaching the disclosure trigger threshold of 5%" (p. 25) with urgency and price pressure concentrated in the last ten days. **Cite this as the antecedent for the run-up/jump split**; do not present the deadline subperiod as new.
3. **The 13D announcement CAR has already been regressed on liquidity, and it came back null.** Table 10 col. 5 (p. 53) includes lagged Amihud illiquidity: **−0.0013 [0.0054]**, insignificant, alongside a significant activist-accumulation term (+0.1723\*\* / +0.1822\*\*). Our empirical leg is a different object (the *split* of the CAR into run-up and jump, interacted with Post-2024), but a referee will point at this null and ask why it did not show up. The honest answer — theirs is a level regression on the filing-day CAR, ours is a composition regression on the run-up share — has to be in the paper, and R12 belongs in the empirical-design file as a magnitude benchmark.
4. **Insider counter-trading contaminates our run-up measurement.** Inside the 60-day window insiders buy an extra 0.21–0.24% of shares outstanding on activist-trade days (R13) and cut net selling by 0.83–0.93 pp (R7). Part of the pre-filing run-up we intend to attribute to pooled-cell inference is *insider* order flow responding to the activist. This is a measurement caveat for the empirics lane (a control or a heterogeneity cut on insider Form 4 activity in the window), not a theoretical refutation.

**What they hand us.** (a) A published statement that the pre-filing period is informationally active and that *someone in the market can tell* — support for a pooled cell with non-degenerate posterior $\pi$ rather than a blind pool. (b) A published magnitude for how visible activist accumulation is (26.3% of turnover in the window, 30.1% in the last ten days, p. 20) — directly usable for calibrating our coarsening $\Gamma$ and the pooled order-flow signal-to-noise. (c) Evidence that the *response* is concentrated in true insiders (R14) and in low-vulnerability firms (R15), i.e. it is genuinely news to the responder — useful when we argue the flag changes information rather than merely confirming it. (d) An unmodelled strategic margin they document but cannot handle (R17: activists time trades into insiders' blackout days) — an example of the kind of within-window timing choice our $T$ makes tractable.
