## 1. Question

Why do activists initiate so many costly campaigns and proxy fights despite the free-rider problem, and why do targets settle so often without a fight? The answer proposed is a **dynamic reputation for proxy fighting**: targets infer the activist's unobserved cost type from their track record, so activists invest in aggression today to extract settlements tomorrow. The paper measures how much of observed activism is reputation-building, and how much value activism would lose without reputation.

## 2. Model / data and method

**Stage game (Model Fig. 1, p. 32).** One long-lived activist A, a sequence of short-lived target managers M, both risk-neutral. Three nodes: A chooses **13-D** (initiate, at private cost `L̃`) or **Ignore**; M chooses **Settle** (undertake the project, private cost `B > Δ`) or **Refuse**; A chooses **Fight** (proxy fight, extra private cost `F̃_A`, always succeeds) or **Fold**. The project raises firm value by `Δ > 0`. Payoffs: `[Δ − L̃, Δ − B]` on Settle; `[Δ − L̃ − F̃_A, Δ − B − F̃_M]` on Fight; `[−L̃, 0]` on Fold.

**Costs are drawn from distributions, not scalars** (p. 32, Q4): `log(L̃) ~ N(μ_L, τ_L^{−2})`, `log(F̃_M/(B−Δ)) ~ N(μ_M, τ_M^{−2})`, `log(F̃_A) ~ N(μ_A, τ_A^{−2})`. A learns `L̃` before choosing 13-D and `F̃_A` only after; M learns `F̃_M` before choosing Settle. **Activists differ only in `μ_A`**, taking two values `μ_agr < μ_caut` — aggressive vs cautious type.

**Dynamics.** Campaign opportunities arrive Poisson at annual rate `λ_c`; annual discount factor `δ`. **The only state variable is reputation `r_t` = P(A is aggressive | observed track record)** (p. 33). M does not see true outcomes: only `13-D` (an activist-day indicator), `Proxy`, and a vector `a` of five noisy target-action indicators with `P(a_i = 1) = â_i + 1(Settle or Fight)·β_i`. `r_t` updates by Bayes' rule after campaigns, decays continuously between them (because silence suggests Ignore), and mean-reverts toward `r_0` via Poisson "type resets" at rate `λ_r`. Equilibrium is five functions of `r_t` (`d_caut`, `d_agr`, `y`, `f_caut`, `f_agr`), solved by value-function iteration on a 106-point `r_t` grid (Appendix A.2, p. 53).

**Accumulation, stake size, price formation — the load-bearing point for us.** There is **no accumulation stage, no stake choice, no order flow, no price function, no disclosure timing**. Buying the stake is folded into the single act "purchasing shares in the target firm and filing a 13-D" (Q3). **Price impact is *named* but not modelled** (corrected by verifier): the introduction says the campaign cost encompasses "the price impact associated with buying shares in the target" (p. 30, Q13), and §3 calls the same thing "round trip liquidity costs" (p. 32, Q1). Both are labels on the scalar draw `L̃` — there is no order flow, no price function and no dependence on any liquidity variable anywhere in the model or the estimation. Position size is not a model object: "For A, this implies that all payoffs are in units of itheir initial investment in the target firm" (p. 32, Q5, typo in the original) — payoffs are *returns on* an unmodelled position. Liquidity enters exactly once, as one of four things bundled into `L̃`: Q1. Estimated mean campaign cost `L̃` = **5.44% of the activist's position**; mean `F̃_A` = 8.68% (aggressive) / 19.44% (cautious) (p. 42). **(added by verifier)** §2 (p. 31) states plainly why the stake is not an object: "The existing literature focuses on a large shareholder of a single firm… In this literature, large shareholders are effective activists because their position sizes reduce the free rider problem" (Q14) — they replace position size with reputation as the source of activist power, naming Maug (1998) and Back et al. (2018) as the literature they depart from.

**Data.** 1999–2016. Start: 35,768 campaigns from 13-D filings on EDGAR (via WRDS SEC Analytics) + 5,910 from SharkWatch → matched/filtered to 4,235 non-overlapping campaigns → main sample **2,434 campaigns by 420 unique hedge funds targeting 1,889 unique firms**, plus **737,004 activist-days** for the 13-D frequency likelihood (Table 1, p. 41). Wolf packs collapsed to a single lead activist (p. 40). Settlement is **never observed**: it is inferred from five activist-friendly action indicators in the year after initiation — `Reorg`, `Payout`, `CEO`, `Board`, `Acq` — each netted against a predicted value `â_i` from quarterly cross-sectional predictive regressions on all CRSP/Compustat firms (Appendix C, p. 54). `CAR` = three-day **[−1, +1]** market-adjusted target return around the initiation date.

**Estimation.** **Maximum likelihood** (not SMM), chosen because a closed-form likelihood exists (p. 32). Ten parameters `θ = {Δ, d_caut,0, τ_L, y_0, τ_M, f_caut,0, f_agr,0, τ_A, r_0, λ_r}`; the μ's are reparameterised as zero-reputation probabilities for interpretability. Per-campaign likelihood factors into a gap term, a 13-D term, a CAR term and an outcome term (Eq. 22, p. 42). Eight further parameters are **fixed, not estimated**: `δ = 0.90` and `λ_c = 10` are *assumed* (they are not separately identified from `τ_L`/`τ_A` and `d_caut,0` respectively); `σ_car = 8.99` is the sample SD of CAR; the five `β_i` are set to the average `a_i − â_i` in proxy campaigns (Table 2 Panel B, p. 43). SEs account for first-stage error in `σ_car` and `β_i`; 95% CIs from likelihood-ratio tests. Reduced-form validation regressions cluster SEs by activist.

## 4. Institutional facts used

- **The 13D ten-day filing window is stated exactly once, in a footnote, as background** — never as a model object: "The SEC requires that investors file a beneficial ownership report on Form 13-D within ten days of initiating an activist campaign." (p. 39, fn. 13 — Q2). The rule's *threshold* (5%) is not stated anywhere in the paper.
- **13-D filing date = campaign initiation date** throughout, both in the model and in the data (Q3; Appendix B.3, p. 54).
- Proxy fights identified from EDGAR contested-solicitation forms DFAN14A, DEFR14A, DEFC14A, DEFN14A, plus SharkWatch's "Proxy Fight" flag (p. 40, fn. 15).
- Activist filter: drop 13-D filers whose CIK has no 13-F filings; drop SharkWatch "Corporate"/"Indiv." filers (p. 40, fn. 14).
- Bebchuk–Brav–Jiang–Keusch (2020): formal legal settlements occur in only 13% of campaigns, motivating the inferred-settlement design (p. 40).
- **Sample is 1999–2016. The 2024-02-05 acceleration to five business days is not mentioned** — it postdates the paper by three years.

## 5. Referee-facing strengths / weaknesses

**Strengths:** a genuinely dynamic structural model with a closed-form likelihood, so identification uses the full joint distribution rather than chosen moments; the reputation measure `r_t` is a *published, reusable* by-product (available on Johnson's website, p. 30 fn. 2). Two nested nulls are formally tested and rejected (R6). The reduced-form regressions in Table 6 are an unusually honest fit diagnostic — they show where the model fails, not only where it works. Robustness spans δ, λ_c, the CAR window, â, random Δ, and φ < 1 (R15).

**Weaknesses / open flanks:**
1. **Settlement is never observed.** The central second-stage outcome is imputed from five action indicators netted against a predicted baseline. `Board` alone carries most of the signal (25.6% vs 0.4% predicted) and the paper concedes the Capital IQ code it uses "refers to activism-driven changes" (p. 41) — i.e. partly mechanical.
2. **Two of the eight fixed parameters are pure assumptions that trade off against estimated ones.** δ = 0.9 is assumed *because* it cannot be told apart from τ_L and τ_A; λ_c = 10 is assumed *because* it cannot be told apart from d_caut,0 (p. 42). The reputation-building shares (R9) are exactly the quantities those parameters govern.
3. **`r_0` = 2.05% with SE 1.94** — the unconditional share of aggressive types is barely distinguishable from zero, and its CI runs [1.00, 9.80].
4. **The model systematically under-predicts the reputation–proxy relation** (R14) and, with activist fixed effects, the CAR and Proxy coefficients are insignificant (0.88, SE 1.19; 7.86, SE 6.62) (Table 6 Panel B).
5. **Headline percentages in the abstract and introduction use shifting bases.** "3.5 times as many campaigns" (abstract, p. 29) compares the high-`r_t` rate 3.50 to the *full-sample* 1.00, while §5.4 says high-`r_t` days initiate campaigns "six times as frequently as the 0.58 per year rate for low rt activist-days" (**p. 46**, corrected by verifier — the card had p. 48). Likewise the intro's "targets settle with high reputation activists 44% of the time, compared to 29% for low reputation activists" and "fight 26% … compared to 14%" (p. 30) line up with Table 5's *full-sample* values (28.55%, 13.74%), not its low-`r_t` values (23.86%, 10.12%). Anyone quoting these must say which base.
6. **All counterfactuals (Table 7) and motivation shares (Table 5) come without standard errors.**
6b. **(added by verifier) Table 3 (p. 45) and Table 5 Panel A (p. 49) disagree on which of 1.00 and 0.93 is the data and which is the model** for the full-sample 13-D frequency. Table 3 reads "Data 1.00 (0.08) / Baseline 0.93"; Table 5 reads "13-D (model) 1.00 / 13-D (data) 0.93". §5.4's text sides with Table 5. Do not cite the full-sample 13-D fit from one table alone.
7. Wolf-pack campaigns are collapsed to one lead activist by a hand-built rule (p. 40); 94% of activists have ≤ 20 campaigns, which the authors show biases fixed-effects regressions downward (p. 49 fn. 20).
8. Only hedge funds in the main sample; the 1,801 non-hedge-fund campaigns are relegated to an Online Appendix not contained in this PDF.

## 6. What they do NOT do (scope boundary)

**On the specific things our positioning stage asked about, be exact:**

- **There is no `σ²T` expression, and no 13-D-window ↔ noise-trading statement, anywhere in this paper.** I ran exhaustive searches over both a fresh `pdftotext` extraction and `research/txt_extracts/johnson-swem-2021-jfe.txt` for "σ", "sigma", "noise trad", "Kyle", "accumulat", "pre-disclosure", "stake", "ten days", "10 days", "window". The **only** σ in the paper is `σ_car`, the standard deviation of the three-day announcement CAR, fixed at 8.99% (Table 2 Panel B, p. 43); the **only** "noise" is the precision of the cost draws and of M's inference; and the **only** 13-D-window statement is footnote 13 (Q2), which is institutional background, not a model object. **Our note that this paper contains a σ²T / window ↔ noise-trading result appears to be misattributed** — that object belongs to Back, Collin-Dufresne, Fos, Li & Ljungqvist (2018, *Econometrica* 86, 1431–1463), "Activism, Strategic Trading, and Liquidity", which Johnson–Swem cite three times (pp. 31, 32, 55) but do not build on. The positioning stage should re-route this question to the Back et al. card.
- **Is the disclosure date `T` fixed?** There is no `T`. Filing is not a timed decision: the model's first move is a single composite action, "initiate a campaign by purchasing shares in the target firm and filing a 13-D" (Q3). Buying and filing are the same instant. There is therefore **no filing delay, no accumulation window, no pre-filing trading, and nothing to endogenise.**
- **Is endogenous `T` declared open?** **No.** The paper's only "future research" paragraph is about *other dimensions of activist heterogeneity* — "Activists in our model differ only by their average cost of proxy fighting… other forms of activist heterogeneity could give rise to reputations for frequent campaigning, identifying high-value projects, negotiating advantageous settlements, and many other skills. Future research could examine these possibilities using a similar approach to this paper." (p. 52, Q7). Disclosure timing is not raised as open; it is simply absent. **Do not claim they leave it open — claim they never model it.**
- **Accumulation / cost-draw structure.** Accumulation: none. Cost draws: **distributions, not scalars** — three independent lognormals per campaign (Q4), which is precisely what generates interior mixed behaviour and lets the paper avoid the Kreps–Wilson pooling equilibrium.
- **Liquidity proxies in estimation: none for the target stock.** (The price-impact mention on p. 30 is rhetorical — nothing in the likelihood varies with it.) `L̃` bundles "round trip liquidity costs" with three other cost categories (Q1) and is never decomposed. The Table 6 Panel C controls are activist-level (`Log Portfolio Size`, `Portfolio Turnover` — the *activist's own* portfolio turnover à la Gaspar et al. 2005) plus `Stake Size`, defined from Form 13-F with a sample average of roughly 7% (Q8). No Amihud, no spread, no volume, no turnover of the target.
- **No takeover premium, no bidder entry, no control outcome beyond campaign success.** `Acq` is one of five binary success indicators; premia are never computed.
- **Identification is structural (MLE) plus descriptive regressions.** No quasi-experiment, no DiD, no policy shock, no rule change.
- **(added from OA by supplement reader) The Online Appendix does not widen the scope by one inch.** Across all 20 pages of the OA: `liquid*` **0 hits**, `price impact` 0, `Kyle` 0, `Amihud` 0, `order flow` 0, `accumulat*` 0, `stake` 0, `toehold` 0, `disclos*` **0**, `takeover` 0, `premium` 0, `tender` 0, `threshold` 0, `five percent` 0, `2024` 0. The only trading-related phrase in the whole OA is **"price pressure by activists"** (OA p. 6, Q15) — again a *label* on unmodelled behaviour, in a robustness paragraph, never a variable.
- **(added from OA by supplement reader) They looked at the pre-filing window twice and both times only as measurement noise, never as economics.** (i) OA App. C.2 (published App. E) re-estimates on a **[−10, +10] 20-day CAR** precisely because it captures "any increase in prices before or after the 13-D filing attributable to price pressure by activists" — and reports that widening the window has "a negligible impact on our parameter estimates and main results" (OA Table 2 col. 8), while the SD of CAR doubles from 9% to 18% (OA p. 6). (ii) OA App. D (published App. F) traces CAR from t−10 to t+252 (R16). **Neither exercise contains a stake, a trade, a filing delay or a liquidity variable** — the pre-filing run-up is treated as a nuisance in the dependent variable, not as an object.

## 7. Implications for our position

**What they occupy.** Object: **campaign frequency, settlement, proxy fights, announcement CAR** — campaign success, not control outcomes. Margin of the disclosure rule: **none** (the ten-day window appears in one background footnote; the 5% threshold is never mentioned). Identification: **structural MLE on 1999–2016 hedge-fund campaigns**, validated by reduced-form regressions on a model-derived regressor.

**How this constrains us.**
1. **The 13-D filing is *the* activism event for this literature, and it is treated as a point in time.** Our contribution is to make that point in time an *interval* — the window margin — and to price what happens inside it. Johnson–Swem is the cleanest citation for how completely that interval is currently collapsed.
2. **Their cost parameter is our benchmark.** Mean campaign cost of 5.44% of the activist's position, against Gantchev's 5.05% (R5) — if our model puts a price on liquidity or on the window, these are the numbers it should be compared to.
3. **They own the reputation channel.** We should not model repeated campaigns or type-learning; that ground is taken and taken well.

**The whitespace they leave, in our vocabulary.**
- **Liquidity (κ) as a driving variable: entirely free.** It is one clause inside a lognormal cost draw (Q1), with no target-stock liquidity proxy in any specification. **(added by verifier) Be precise when we write this up: they *name* price impact as a cost of buying the stake (p. 30, Q13) — so the sentence to write is "named as a cost, never a variable", not "absent". A referee with the paper open will check.**
- **The window margin: free — but the motivation is weaker than this card claimed. (revised from OA by supplement reader.)** The old wording leaned R16 on "leakage". The OA's own decomposition (OA App. D, OA p. 10, Q16) splits the missing effect as **~1 pp before** the window and **~2.5 pp after** it. So the *larger* share of what falls outside [−1,+1] is **delayed reaction, not leakage**, and pre-announcement run-up is the *smallest* of the three pieces. Three consequences for how we write this up: (a) never write "the effect happens before the filing" — write "only around a third occurs in the announcement window, with a smaller pre-announcement and a larger post-announcement component"; (b) the estimate is a **coefficient read off a figure with a 90% band** (OA Fig. 1) — no number is tabulated, so do not quote a coefficient or an SE, only the paper's own sentence (Q16); (c) the effect is per unit of `r_t` ∈ [0,1], i.e. it is the r=0 → r=1 contrast, not a per-campaign average. **The whitespace still stands** — nothing in the OA models what happens inside the pre-filing interval; it just is not the smoking gun the card previously made it.
- **(added from OA by supplement reader) Their own robustness run is the cleanest evidence that the window is not their object.** They widen the CAR window to [−10, +10] *for the express purpose of catching activist price pressure around the filing*, and find "a negligible impact" (OA p. 6, OA Table 2 col. 8). A structural model of activism is insensitive to whether the pre-filing accumulation period is in the return window — because nothing in the model generates behaviour there. That is our object stated as their non-result.
- **The threshold margin: free.** 5% is never mentioned. **(added by verifier) The word "disclosure" does not occur anywhere in the 28-page article** — not in the model, the data section, the appendices or the reference titles.
- **The partition: free.** There is no pooled state in their model; filing and buying are simultaneous, so the market is never uncertain about whether disclosure has occurred.
- **Control outcomes (bidder entry, takeover premium): free.** `Acq` is a binary success dummy; no price, no premium.
- **Design-based identification and the Feb-2024 acceleration: free.** Sample ends 2016.
- **Useful complementarity, not conflict.** Their `r_t` is publicly available per campaign. If our empirical leg ever needs an activist-level control that is not a liquidity or window variable, `r_t` is an off-the-shelf, referee-recognised one — cite it as a control, not a competitor object.

**One-line index.** JFE 139 (2021) 29–56 · object: campaign frequency, settlement and proxy-fight success (no premium, no control outcome) · margin: none (ten-day window in one background footnote; 5% threshold never mentioned) · identification: structural MLE, 2,434 hedge-fund campaigns 1999–2016 · so-what: the 13-D filing is a point, not an interval — the window margin, the partition and liquidity are all untouched, and their own leakage admission (only ~1/3 of the reputation effect is inside [−1,+1]) motivates our window.
