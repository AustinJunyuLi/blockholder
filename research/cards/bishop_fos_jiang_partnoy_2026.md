## 1. Question

Does antitrust law — specifically the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) premerger notification regime — deter shareholder activism? The authors argue HSR was written for mergers, not for minority stake-building, but that its dollar notification threshold plus its 30-day waiting period bite on activists building a toehold, and can bite **before** the Schedule 13D 5% threshold does. They then ask the reverse policy question regulators have raised: does activism itself raise industry concentration (which would justify the antitrust hook)? They answer no. The paper's purpose is explicitly policy: to support changing "the HSR premerger notification rules, including its threshold and investment-only exception" (abstract, PDF p. 2).

Note the paper has **no model**: no primitives, no equilibrium, no proposition, no proof. It is institutional description (§2, printed pp. 3–22, roughly half the paper) plus six regression tables (§3, printed pp. 22–33) plus a policy discussion (§4, printed pp. 34–37).

## 2. Model / data and method

**No theory.** There is no formal model anywhere in the paper.

**The institutional lever (HSR premerger notification) — as the paper states it:**
- **What it is.** HSR (Pub. L. 94-435, 90 Stat. 1383, 15 U.S.C. § 18a; enacted 1976) requires parties to an acquisition above certain **absolute dollar** size thresholds to file a premerger notification with the FTC and to **observe a waiting period before the transaction may lawfully be consummated** (printed p. 3).
- **Dollar level.** The statutory minimum notification threshold is **$50 million (as adjusted)** subject to a size-of-person test; a filing is required regardless of party size above **$200 million (as adjusted)** (printed pp. 4–5). The threshold is **adjusted annually by GNP growth** since 2005 (fn. 1, printed p. 5). As of **21 Feb 2025** the adjusted minimum is **$126.4 million** (printed p. 5). Precise 2025 bands, fn. 2, printed p. 5: transactions above $126.4m but below $505.8m require a filing only under the size-of-person test (one party ≥ $25.3m, the other ≥ $252.9m); above $505.8m the size-of-transaction test alone suffices. **Table 1 (printed pp. 23–24)** prints the full threshold path used in the regressions: $50m (from 1 Feb 2000), $53.1m, $56.7m, $59.8m, $63.1m, $65.2m, $63.4m, $66m, $68.2m, $70.9m, $75.9m, $76.3m, $78.2m, $80.8m, $84.4m, $90m, $94m, $92m, $101m, $111.4m, $119.5m, $126.4m.
- **It covers open-market minority buying, not just mergers.** "The HSR Act requires reporting not only for whole-company acquisitions, but also partial stock purchases, such as cash tender offers or purchases of an issuer's shares on a national exchange" (printed p. 4). It applies to acquisitions of voting securities and **aggregates previously acquired holdings** of that issuer (printed p. 5).
- **Filing rule / who is told, and when.** Both the acquiring **and the acquired** person must file, even where the acquirer buys from a third party such as an exchange (16 C.F.R. pt. 801.30[a]); the waiting period starts when the bidder files, and **the target must file 15 days after the bidder (10 days for a cash tender offer)** (16 C.F.R. pts. 801.30[b], 803.10[a]) (printed p. 6). Critically: "HSR therefore requires an investor to serve notice on the issuer before acquiring additional shares to ensure the issuer is aware of its reporting obligations (16 C.F.R. 803.5[a])" (printed p. 6).
- **Waiting period.** **30 days** from filing; **15 days** for an all-cash tender offer or a bankruptcy acquisition (printed p. 3). A "second request" extends it a further 30 days (10 days for a cash tender offer or bankruptcy sale) from substantial compliance (printed p. 4). Early Termination may cut it short; the Early Termination notice is **published on the FTC's website one day later** (printed p. 4).
- **Publicity.** "HSR filings are exempt from public disclosure (15 U.S.C. 18a[h])" (printed p. 5). **So the HSR lever is a compelled disclosure to the issuer and to the agencies plus a timing delay — not a public flag.** The only public leak channel the paper names is the Early Termination notice (printed p. 4).
- **Costs.** Filing fees $30,000 to more than $2 million by transaction size (printed p. 5). Civil penalty for non-compliance **$53,088 per day** effective 17 Jan 2025 (printed p. 3).
- **Why it precedes 13D.** Because the threshold is in **dollars**, not percent, at a large-cap firm a **sub-5%** stake can exceed it. See Q3 below and §4 here.
- **The "investment-only" exception (15 U.S.C. § 18a[c][9])** exempts acquisitions "solely for the purpose of investment" of up to **10%** of outstanding voting securities (printed p. 10), but the FTC reads it narrowly: it applies only if the investor has "no intention of participating in the formulation, determination, or direction of the basic business decisions of the issuer" (16 C.F.R. pt. 801.1[i][1], printed p. 10). Six enumerated conduct types defeat it, including **nominating a board candidate** (fn. 6, printed p. 11). The FTC proposed a blanket de-minimis exemption in 1988 and again in 2020 and adopted neither (printed pp. 12–13); the HSR Notification Form update effective **10 Feb 2025** tightens the position for 801.30 (open-market/hostile) acquisitions where the acquirer obtains board-related rights (printed p. 14).

**Design 1 — HSR effect on activist targeting (§3.1, printed pp. 22–28).**
- **Sample:** all activist events in **SharkRepellent** with a campaign announcement date or a Schedule 13D filed from **1 February 2000** (when the HSR threshold was set at $50m) **through end-2024**, merged to **CRSP** monthly market capitalisation.
- **Unit:** firm-month. **N = 1,268,783** (1,268,695 in firm-FE columns).
- **Outcome:** `ActivistCampaign(n,t)` = 1 if an activist campaign is announced at firm n in month t.
- **Treatment:** `HSR Indicator(n,t)` = 1 if **5% of firm n's market capitalisation at t exceeds the (as-adjusted) HSR threshold at t**, i.e. if a 5% toehold would itself be an HSR-reportable acquisition. Lagged one month in the regression.
- **Running variable:** `Mkt_Cap_Control(n,t) = ln(Mkt_Cap(n,t−1)) − ln($50,000,000)` (Eq. 1, printed p. 25) — i.e. **centred at $50 million market cap**, not at the discontinuity.
- **Specification:** linear probability model, Eq. (2) printed p. 25 (level control only) and Eq. (3) printed p. 27 (cubic polynomial in the running variable **fully interacted** with HSR Indicator, following "the guidance of Angrist and Pischke (2008) at Equation 6.1.6"). Four columns each: no FE / firm FE / year FE / both. **SEs double-clustered by firm and year.**
- **Identification claim:** a discontinuity in targeting probability at the HSR threshold, over and above a smooth relation with firm size (Q7).

**Design 2 — activism and industry concentration (§3.2, printed pp. 28–33).**
- **Data:** Compustat, all SIC industries with more than one firm (HHI not mechanically 10,000), **2000–2024, quarterly**. HHI = sum of squared market shares (printed p. 4).
- **(a) Two-way FE (Eq. 4, printed pp. 29–30 — the equation closes printed p. 29, its notation is defined at the top of printed p. 30):** `HHI(i,t)` on `AfterActivism(i,t)` (any activist engagement in industry i within 1/2/3 years), industry and year FE. **N = 41,402.**
- **(b) Intensity (Eq. 5, printed p. 31):** `HHI(i,t)` on `NumberOfCampaigns(i,t)`, optionally controlling for `Companies(i,t)`. **N = 41,402.**
- **(c) DiD "event study" (Eq. 6, printed p. 32):** treated = industry with activism within 1/2/3 years; **control = an industry in the same three-digit SIC code with no activism in that window**, with the requirement that each treated industry have at least one same-3-digit-SIC control. Regressors: Treatment, After, Treatment × After, industry and year FE. **N = 13,994 / 18,054 / 20,746** for 1/2/3-year windows. SEs double-clustered by industry and year.

## 4. Institutional facts used

**HSR (the paper's lever)** — dollar threshold path (Table 1, printed pp. 23–24), $50m/$200m statutory minima and 2025 bands $126.4m/$505.8m/$25.3m/$252.9m (printed pp. 4–5), 30-day waiting period, 15 days for all-cash tender offers, +30/+10 days on a second request (printed pp. 3–4), acquirer-and-target dual filing with target filing 15 days (10 for cash tender offer) after the bidder (printed p. 6), notice to the issuer under 16 C.F.R. 803.5[a] (printed p. 6), filings exempt from public disclosure (printed p. 5), Early Termination notice published on the FTC website one day later (printed p. 4), fees $30,000–$2m+ and $53,088/day penalties (printed pp. 3, 5), investment-only 10% exemption and its narrow FTC reading (printed pp. 8–14), 1988 and 2020 abandoned de-minimis rulemakings (printed pp. 12–13), the 12 Nov 2024 / 10 Feb 2025 HSR Form tightening (printed p. 14), and enforcement actions: **Biglari $1.4m (Oct 2022)** plus **$850,000 (2012)**, **Third Point $600,000 (Dec 2019)** and an injunctive-only Yahoo settlement (Aug 2015), **ValueAct $11m (Jul 2016)** on $2.5bn of Halliburton/Baker Hughes stock, **KKR (Jan 2025)**, maximum authorised penalties exceeding $650 million (printed pp. 20–22).

**Schedule 13D / Williams Act (used only as an institutional fact, never as a treatment):**
- **The 5% threshold.** Section 13(d) requires disclosure of blockholdings above 5% — quoted in full at Q4. It is used in the empirics **only to define what a "toehold" is** (Q6): `HSR Indicator = 1` iff 5% of market cap > HSR threshold. The 13D threshold is therefore a **measurement convention here, not a treatment**.
- **The filing window.** The paper states the **current** rule as "within five business days of surpassing the five-percent-threshold" (printed p. 18) and describes the change in fn. 15 (Q5): "shortening the window for disclosure of five-percent stakes to five business days, from ten calendar days". **On the Feb-2024 acceleration specifically: the paper never states the 2024-02-05 effective date, never states the 13D/A 2-business-day rule, never mentions 13G at all, and never uses the change as an event.** Its only citations are SEC 2023 (the 10 Oct 2023 adoption press release) and 88 Fed. Reg. 76,896 (7 Nov 2023). The word "ten calendar days" also appears in the implications (printed p. 36, Q11). Note the paper says the pre-2024 window was **ten calendar days**, which matches the old rule.
- **Toehold-size margin.** "a typical activist will purchase a sizeable but ultimately limited minority position—normally between 5% and 9.9% of outstanding shares—before attempting to improve the performance of the targeted firm" (printed p. 17) — cited to Brav et al. 2008a/b, **not measured here**.
- **Other ceilings on the toehold** (fn. 14, printed p. 17): Delaware-blessed low-trigger poison pills at 10%; Section 16 disclosure of trades within 48 hours and short-swing disgorgement above 10%.
- **Pre-disclosure accumulation.** Described as the activist's whole profit source (Q12), with 13D filings associated with "large, positive average abnormal returns" (Brav et al. 2008a; Albuquerque, Fos, and Schroth 2022) (printed p. 18). Stealth mechanics: prime brokers, OTC cash-settled equity swaps (Collin-Dufresne, Fos & Muravyev 2021; Bebchuk 2022), and the worked **Pershing Square / Chipotle** case — 9.9% announced 6 Sep 2016, forward purchase contracts for 2,328,250 shares at a net purchase price of $974,342,953, physical settlement conditioned on HSR clearance, early termination granted 17 Oct 2016, physical receipt 20 Oct 2016 (printed pp. 19–20).

**(added by verifier) What the paper says about *control* and *campaign success* — in prose, printed pp. 16–18.** Material to us because it is their own stated mechanism and they never estimate it:
- "Unlike private equity firms, hedge fund activists normally do not seek control of the firms in which they invest (Gilson and Gordon 2013)" (printed pp. 16–17). Their object is deliberately *not* a control transfer.
- "due to the small size of their stakes, activist hedge funds cannot win proxy fights (or plausibly threaten them) without the support of other shareholders", and those shareholders are the Big Three, "which have largely outsourced the work of corporate monitoring to activist hedge funds" (printed p. 17). So campaign success is, on their own account, a function of the shareholder base, not of the toehold.
- "activists identify target companies based in part on whether they believe that company's shareholders will support an insurgent" (Brav et al. 2021; Bishop 2021, printed p. 18) — a **selection-into-targeting channel** that is not in their specification and would be in ours.

**(added by verifier) The "investment-only" test is a *purpose* partition enforced by reading communications — printed pp. 35–36.** "the Agencies have based their determinations of whether an activist's engagement meets the 'investment-only' exemption by looking into the activist's communications with management (see, for example, FTC [2024b], describing HSR enforcement action against activist investor who communicated with management of issuer to advocate for a board seat)." This is the HSR analogue of the 13D/13G purpose split, and it is *ex post* and discretionary rather than a bright line. Relevant to our partition identity, and to Corum's α₀′/α₁′ neutralisation: here the purpose flag is load-bearing enough to draw a $600,000–$11m penalty.

**(added by verifier) Activism and M&A, printed p. 35 — prose only.** They concede activists "sometimes take stakes in public companies in order to influence merger and acquisition activity", either to force a sale (Swanson, Young & Yust 2022; Boyson, Gantchev & Shivdasani 2017) or to curb deals (Jiang, Li & Mei 2019; Gantchev, Sevilir & Shivdasani 2020), and answer that "the FTC and DOJ already may weigh in on the merger itself". This is the closest the paper comes to a control outcome and it is entirely narrative — no deal, bid or premium variable is built.

**Liquidity: absent.** The strings "liquid", "Amihud", "volume" and "price impact" return **zero hits** in all 46 pages. *(corrected by verifier: "turnover" has exactly **one** hit, and it is a reference title — Jiang, Wei. 2022. "Diversity Through Turnover…", printed p. 43. Nothing in the body, no variable.)* There is no trading-volume, depth, spread or noise-trading variable in any specification.

**Takeover / premium objects: absent from the empirics.** "premium"/"premia" appears **once**, in the implications, as a literature reference: bidders with pre-bid toeholds have different incentives "with implications for bid premia, competition, and deal outcomes (see Betton and Eckbo 2000; Betton, Eckbo, and Thorburn 2009)" and "whether toeholds bind is not clear (see Goldman and Qian, 2005)" (printed p. 36). ## 5. Referee-facing strengths / weaknesses

**Strengths**
- The institutional half (§2) is genuinely excellent and citable: it is the cleanest single account we have seen of why an antitrust dollar threshold interacts with securities-law percentage thresholds in stake-building, complete with the primary Fed. Reg. and legislative-history citations.
- The lever is a real, dated, mechanical, non-securities-law friction with an annually-adjusted dollar cutoff (Table 1) — cross-sectional **and** time-series variation, and variation the activist cannot manipulate at the moment of targeting.
- Huge sample (1.27m firm-months, 2000–2024), plausible clustering, sign stable across all four FE combinations and both specifications.
- The concentration section is admirably honest about not being causal (Q8, Q9) — a referee cannot accuse them of overclaiming there.

**Weaknesses / open flanks**
- **The running variable is not centred at the discontinuity.** `Mkt_Cap_Control = ln(MktCap) − ln($50m)` (Eq. 1, printed p. 25), but `HSR Indicator` switches when `0.05 × MktCap > threshold`, i.e. at `MktCap > 20 × threshold` — **$1.0 bn in 2000, $2.528 bn in 2025**. In the units of the running variable, the cutoff sits at `ln(20) ≈ 3.00` in 2000 and drifts to `ln(20) + ln(126.4/50) ≈ 3.92` by 2025. So (i) the cubic-with-interaction coefficient in Table 3 is the gap between the two fitted polynomials **evaluated at Mkt_Cap_Control = 0** — roughly three to four log points away from where treatment actually switches, and outside the treated support — and (ii) a single polynomial in that variable cannot be a local approximation around a cutoff that **moves over time**. *(Reader's inference from the paper's own definitions on printed pp. 23 and 25, not a claim the paper makes. **Arithmetic re-derived and CONFIRMED by the verifier**: HSR Indicator = 1 ⟺ 0.05·MktCap > threshold ⟺ MktCap > 20 × threshold; in 2000 that is $1.0bn and ln(1.0e9) − ln(5.0e7) = ln 20 = 2.996; in 2025 it is $2.528bn and ln(2.528e9) − ln(5.0e7) = 3.923 = ln 20 + ln(126.4/50). The inference stands and remains an inference.)* **(added by verifier) The authors half-anticipate this, in fn. 18, printed p. 25:** "In unreported analyses, all tests remain statistically significant at either the 5% or 1% level using ln(Mkt_Cap) as the (non-centered) control variable. We present results with a centered control variable for ease of interpretation." That defuses nothing for Table 3: in a **fully-interacted** polynomial the HSR coefficient *is* the treated–untreated gap evaluated where the running variable equals zero, so un-centring moves the evaluation point from $50m market cap to $1 — further from the cutoff, not nearer. For Table 2's level spec, centring is genuinely innocuous and the footnote is a fair answer.
- Consistent with that: the Table 3 coefficient (**−0.0715**, i.e. 7 percentage points of monthly targeting probability) is an order of magnitude larger than Table 2's (**−0.0034**) and is implausible against any plausible base rate. With no summary-statistics table and no reported mean of `ActivistCampaign`, **the economic magnitude cannot be benchmarked at all.**
- **No RD apparatus**: no bandwidth, no local-linear estimate, no density/McCrary test for bunching just below the threshold (which is exactly the manipulation the paper's own story predicts), no covariate-balance test, no placebo cutoff, no binned scatter. **The paper contains zero figures.**
- Firm size is the running variable and is also the single strongest predictor of being targeted; Table 2's Mkt Cap Control **flips sign** between column (3) and column (4) (R2), which is a warning that the size control is doing unstable work.
- The word "causal" is asserted (abstract; printed p. 29) on the strength of a polynomial-control OLS. A referee will ask why not a proper RD, and why the discontinuity is never plotted.
- **The mechanism is never tested.** Nothing measures HSR filings, toehold sizes, accumulation speed, or costs. The claim that HSR binds the toehold margin (R10) is inferred entirely from the legal text.
- §3.2 (concentration) is only loosely connected to §3.1; the "same three-digit SIC, no activism" control group is not defended against selection, and the authors say so themselves (Q8).
- No robustness section, no appendix, no placebo, no pre-trend plot for the DiD.
- Typos in the printed version (e.g. "a significantly time delay cost", printed p. 6; "we address the concerned", printed p. 26; "activists targed poorly performing firms", printed p. 34; a 2016 Pershing filing dated "Oct. 21, 2014", printed p. 20) suggest an early draft.

## 6. What they do NOT do (scope boundary)

- **No causal claim on concentration.** "We are careful in this section not to draw causal conclusions, particularly given the complexity of identifying the potential relationship between an appropriate control group and trends in industry consolidation" (printed p. 29, Q8) and "Again, we are careful not to make causal claims about the effects of activism on industry concentration" (printed p. 34, Q9).
- **No liquidity.** No liquidity, volume, turnover, spread or price-impact object appears anywhere.
- **No control outcome.** No takeover premium, no bidder entry, no bid competition, no campaign/proxy-contest success, no announcement return, no ROA or operating outcome is estimated. The only outcomes are (i) an indicator that a campaign is announced and (ii) industry HHI.
- **No toehold-size data.** Despite the paper being *about* toeholds, no actual stake size, accumulation path, or 13D-reported holding is measured. The 5% "toehold" is a definitional bound, not an observed quantity.
- **No test of the 13D window margin.** The Feb-2024 acceleration is invoked only as narrative in the implications (Q11) and fn. 15 (Q5); no event window, no sample split, no estimate. The 2024-02-05 effective date, the 13D/A two-business-day rule and 13G are never mentioned.
- **No structural or theoretical work.** No model, no welfare, no counterfactual.
- **No test of the investment-only exception itself**, despite recommending its reform — no data on which filings claimed it or which were challenged.

**Their HSR lever versus our two 13D margins — the comparison a referee will want.**
1. **Dollars vs percent.** HSR's cutoff is a **dollar** amount on the value of the stake ($50m → $126.4m, Table 1); 13D's is a **percentage** of shares (5%). That is why HSR "can bind even before the traditional 5% 13D threshold" (Q10) at large caps: 5% of a $3bn firm is $150m, above the 2025 threshold. **Their threshold is therefore effectively a firm-size-varying threshold margin**, and it binds only in large caps.
2. **Private notice vs public flag.** HSR filings "are exempt from public disclosure" (printed p. 5) and the required notice runs **to the issuer** (16 C.F.R. 803.5[a], printed p. 6) and the agencies. **The market is not informed.** Our partition is exactly the opposite: 13D makes the stake and its purpose *public*, which is what creates the flagged/pooled split and the price response. HSR is a **management-warning + delay** friction; 13D is an **information-revelation** friction. This is the single sharpest line to draw: **they study a friction that does not move the market's information set; we study one that does.**
3. **Waiting period vs filing window — opposite signs on time.** HSR imposes a **30-day freeze before** the acquirer may buy more (printed p. 3, printed p. 6, Q2). The 13D window is a **grace period after** crossing 5% during which the acquirer may keep buying quietly. HSR's clock **stops** accumulation; 13D's clock **permits** it. A shortened 13D window and a longer HSR waiting period both shrink the achievable toehold, but through different channels: one shortens the *stealth interval*, the other shortens the *purchasable interval*. Our window margin has no analogue in their paper.
4. **They pre-empt only the targeting object.** If we ever framed our empirics as "a disclosure friction reduces the probability a firm is targeted", we would be second. We should not. Their own text hands us the demarcation: their contribution is toeholds/targeting, and the takeover-premium literature is cited (Betton–Eckbo, Goldman–Qian) as *context* they do not enter (printed p. 36).

**What they leave open — the whitespace, in their own words.**
- **The window margin is asserted, never modelled or measured.** Q11 — "A shorter disclosure window compresses the time during which activists can accumulate shares quietly, limiting feasible toeholds above 5%" — is precisely the comparative static our core model produces, stated by four senior authors as an unproved conjecture in a policy paragraph. **Our theory supplies what they assert.** This is a strong, citable motivation sentence and it is safe to quote because it is labelled ASSERTED.
- **Liquidity is completely absent.** No paper-level obstacle exists to our κ dimension; nothing here overlaps.
- **Control outcomes are completely absent.** Bidder entry, premium and campaign success are untouched.
- **The Feb-2024 acceleration is unexploited.** They do not use it, do not date it, and do not mention 13D/A or 13G. Our anchor is intact.
- **(added by verifier) They pre-frame the two frictions as joint — in one sentence we can cite.** Printed p. 36: "Taken together, the dual effects of HSR thresholds and the accelerated 13D timeline highlight how overlapping regulatory regimes jointly curtail the economics of activism." Four senior authors state that the HSR threshold and the 2024 window acceleration operate on the same margin *at the same time*, without separating them. That is simultaneously (i) the best available motivation sentence for separating them, and (ii) the reason a referee will ask us to.
- **(added by verifier) Their own mechanism runs through the shareholder base, not the toehold.** Printed pp. 16–18: activists "normally do not seek control", "cannot win proxy fights … without the support of other shareholders", and pick targets partly on whether the shareholder base backs insurgents. If our control outcome is campaign success, this is the omitted variable they name and do not use; if it is bidder entry or premium, it is the reason their targeting result does not travel to our object.
- **They also supply a confound we must control for.** Their result implies that **firm size interacts with a binding dollar disclosure threshold in a way that changes activist targeting**. If our Feb-2024 window-margin design uses large-cap targets, the HSR threshold is a *live, time-varying, size-dependent* selection channel operating in the same sample and over the same years. It also gives us a ready **placebo/heterogeneity split**: firms where 5% of market cap is far below the HSR threshold face the 13D window margin without the HSR waiting period; firms above face both. We should add "HSR-binding" to our confound list.

**Deliverability note.** Their data are SharkRepellent + CRSP + Compustat. Nothing here requires a tool we do not have; the HSR threshold path is publicly printed in their Table 1 and reproducible from the Federal Register. If we want the HSR interaction as a control or a split, it costs one merge.

**Risk to us.** Two of the four authors (Fos, Jiang) are in our competitor set's orbit (Albuquerque–Fos–Schroth; Collin-Dufresne–Fos; Brav–Jiang) and are plausible referees. They will know this literature exactly and will expect our paper to cite this one and to state — in one sentence — why the HSR result does not subsume ours. The sentence is: *their friction is a private notice plus a purchase freeze that shifts which firms get targeted; ours is a public partition that shifts what a target is worth once engaged.*
