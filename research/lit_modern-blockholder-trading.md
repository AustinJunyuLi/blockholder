## Paper 1: Edmans (2009), "Blockholder Trading, Market Efficiency, and Managerial Myopia"

**Citation & access.** Edmans, Alex, 2009, *Journal of Finance* 64(6), 2481–2513. Accessed as the published JF version (33 pp.) from the author's site: https://alexedmans.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Blockholders.pdf. The Internet Appendix link (faculty.london.edu/aedmans/BAppendix.pdf) is dead, but all main-text results carry their own proofs.

**Research question.** Can a blockholder who *cannot* intervene (no voice) still govern? Yes: informed trading ("exit"/"Wall Street Rule") impounds fundamental value into prices, which shelters managers from short-term earnings pressure and thus encourages long-term investment. The paper inverts the Porter (1992)/Bhide (1993) claim that liquid, transient U.S. markets cause myopia.

**Model architecture.** Three-period Kyle variant, single blockholder B owning α of one share:
- t=1: public signal s ∈ {sg, sb} about V ∈ {0, X}; after sb, V = 0 or X with equal probability (binary firm value).
- t=2: B chooses monitoring effort μ ∈ [0,1] at cost ½cμ²; signal precision Pr(ig|X) = ½ + μ/2. Then trading: B either sells β ∈ [0, α] (**short-sale constraint**: β ≤ α — the load-bearing institutional friction) or holds; noise traders u ~ **exponential(λ)** with λ = 1/(ν(1−α)), ν = liquidity parameter. Market maker sees total demand d = b + u and sets P = E[V | d, s] (competitive, Kyle-style).
- t=3: V revealed.
- Methodological contribution (flagged by the author): **exponential noise + binary value** delivers the informed trade in closed form — Kyle (1985) needs normal/normal; most CF applications with binary V must fix trades exogenously. This is *the* tractability trick of the paper.
- Section II adds a manager: weight ω on t=2 price, 1−ω on t=3 value; a high-quality manager picks investment θ that adds gθ to value but risks emitting sb with probability θ/2. Investment opportunity is *unanticipated* by the market and B (contrast with intervention models where the monitor must know the action).

**Key results (ALL analytic, closed-form; no numerics):**
- **Lemma 1** (equilibrium): two-price schedule — P = πbX if d ≤ 0 (sale detected), P = πmX if d > 0, with πm = [1 + e^(−λβ) + μ(1 − e^(−λβ))] / [2(1 + e^(−λβ))]; monitoring μ = βX/4c; sale volume **β = min(1/λ, α)**. Without the short-sale constraint the unconstrained optimum is 1/λ (finite because of price impact).
- **Lemma 2**: effort and sale volume are **hump-shaped in block size**, maximized at α* = ν/(ν+1). Larger blocks help up to the point where the stake itself destroys liquidity.
- **Proposition 1 (Market efficiency)**: price efficiency πX (closeness of E[P|X] to X) is maximized at α*; decomposition into *trading effect* (positive iff α < α*), *camouflage effect* (positive always), *effort effect* (sign = ∂µ/∂α). Above α*, trading and camouflage effects cancel exactly, leaving the negative effort effect. Contrast with Shleifer–Vishny/Maug/Kahn–Winton where value is monotone in block size.
- **Lemma 3 / Proposition 2 (Investment)**: θ = min{ (1−ω)g / [2ωX(1−πX)], 1 }; investment is increasing in price efficiency, hence hump-shaped in α with the same α*. Cross-partials: blockholder value-added falls in c (information already in market) and ω (myopia), rises in g up to satiation.
- **Proposition 3 (Liquidity, exogenous block)** — the result most relevant to us: with α fixed, market efficiency and investment are **hump-shaped in liquidity ν**, maximized at ν* = α/(1−α). Zero liquidity ⇒ no trading/monitoring; infinite liquidity ⇒ trades fully camouflaged, no price impact. *"The power of loyalty relies on the threat of exit."*
- **Proposition 4 (Liquidity, endogenous block)**: if B chooses her stake to maximize trading profits, α*_P < α* and α*_P rises with ν; with endogenous stake adjustment investment becomes **monotone increasing in ν** (echoing Maug 1998's block-formation channel, but through trading rather than intervention).
- Mechanism contrast the paper stresses: in intervention models liquidity is harmful because voice and exit are mutually exclusive; here liquid trading *is* the governance mechanism, so liquidity from low levels is beneficial even with fixed α.

**Functional forms that load the results:** exponential noise (closed-form β and the e^(−λβ) detection probabilities); binary V (two-price schedule); quadratic monitoring cost (µ = βX/4c); linear-in-θ investment risk (θ/2 probability of sb). The author is explicit that the economic idea (block size → feasible sale volume → monitoring incentive) is functional-form free, but every closed form above leans on the exponential.

**Institutional facts used/documented:** Section 13(d) 5% filing threshold; 10% insider classification (both cited as reasons block size may not respond to liquidity); Holderness (2009): 96% of U.S. firms have a 5% blockholder; La Porta et al. (1999): 20%/10% of large/medium U.S. firms have a 20% blockholder; McCahery, Sautner & Starks survey — exit is the most-used governance mechanism; legal barriers to intervention (Black 1990, Becht et al. 2009); Parrino, Sias & Starks (2003) — blockholders sell ahead of forced CEO turnover; Fang, Noe & Tice (2009) — causal liquidity→firm-value link via price efficiency; Bushee & Goodman (2007) — information content of trades rises in stake size.

**Referee-facing strengths:** genuine methodological innovation (exponential noise) that unlocked a whole literature; clean reframing of the liquidity debate; internally consistent set of testable predictions later borne out. **Weaknesses a referee would attack:** (i) the manager's price concern ω is exogenous — the model solves myopia but assumes it; (ii) single blockholder, single trading round — no feedback from price to B's action within the round, no dynamics; (iii) two-price equilibrium is a pooling artifact of exponential noise — with other noise distributions the inference is smoother and comparative statics need not be signed in closed form; (iv) monitoring effort is unconstrained by any agency problem on B's side; (v) "myopia is rational" only because the manager's contract is taken as given.

**Implications for repositioning our paper:**
- *Cite as the canonical "liquidity hump with fixed position" antecedent.* Our R1 (Δ^min hump-shaped in κ) has the same logical skeleton as Edmans's Prop 3: with position limits, liquidity first enables then camouflages. We should explicitly say: Edmans (2009) gets the hump from the short-sale cap α; we get it from the disclosure-triggered buy constraint and the bidder-entry channel — and we add the disclosure-attenuation theorem (R2), which has no counterpart in Edmans.
- *Borrow the tractability philosophy*: pick a noise law that makes the *indifference/detection probabilities* closed form (his e^(−λβ); our ternary z with p0 = 1−κ, p1 = κ/2 already has this flavor). Edmans shows a two-point price schedule is publishable at JF if the comparative statics are transparent.
- *Differentiate on endogenous voice*: Edmans's blockholder cannot intervene at all; ours chooses voice intensity (Quiet vs Public). The natural framing: "Edmans (2009) shows exit governs; we show the exit–voice margin itself is governed by disclosure-triggered observability."
- *Kill/avoid*: do not mimic the exogenous-ω device; our bidder-entry channel (price → takeover) is a stronger, less attacked microfoundation for why prices matter for real outcomes.

## Paper 2: Edmans & Manso (2011), "Governance Through Trading and Intervention: A Theory of Multiple Blockholders"

**Citation & access.** Edmans, Alex, and Gustavo Manso, 2011, *Review of Financial Studies* 24(7), 2395–2428. Accessed as the ECGI Finance WP 225/2008 (December 2011 version, "(RFS, forthcoming)" on title page): https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finaledmansmanso.pdf. Read in full (37 pp.); appendices referenced but the published online appendices were not separately fetched.

**Research question.** Why are most firms held by *multiple small* blockholders when free-rider logic says one big block governs best? Answer: splitting a block weakens intervention (voice) but strengthens governance-through-trading, because blockholders who cannot coordinate trade competitively (Cournot), impound more information into prices, and thereby discipline a stock-price-motivated manager. There is an interior optimal number of blockholders.

**Model architecture.** Two stages, I symmetric blockholders each holding β/I (β fixed — the paper deliberately varies *number*, not total concentration), manager owns α, free float fixed:
- Firm value ṽ = φa log(1+a) + φb log(1+Σbi) + η̃, η ~ N(0, ση²). Manager action a (cost a, private); blockholder actions bi (cost bi, public in core model). Log production + linear cost chosen precisely so that splitting a block does not mechanically change the monitoring technology.
- Trading stage = **multi-insider Kyle (1985; Kyle 1984, Holden–Subrahmanyam)**: each blockholder observes ṽ perfectly (costly information acquisition in §4.1), submits market order xi(ṽ); noise ε̃ ~ N(0, σε²) — σε is "liquidity"; market maker sets p = E[ṽ | ỹ] on total order flow.
- Backward induction; unique symmetric linear equilibrium in the trading stage; log/normal structure chosen for closed forms.

**Key results (all analytic):**
- **Prop 1 (Trading equilibrium)**: xi = γ(ṽ − conjectured mean), p = mean + λỹ, with **λ = [√I/(I+1)]·(ση/σε)**, γ = (1/√I)(σε/ση); per-blockholder trading profits = σησε/[√I(I+1)], falling in I (Cournot competition).
- **Prop 2 (Price informativeness)**: dp/dv = **I/(I+1)** — a single blockholder reveals only half her information (Kyle monopoly); informativeness rises with I purely through competition, not through more information. **Liquidity σε has NO effect on price informativeness** in the core model (standard Kyle neutrality: more noise → more aggressive informed trading, exact offset).
- **Prop 3 (Actions)**: manager a = φa·α·[I/(I+1)] − 1 — effort rises in I because price informativeness is the ex-post reward/punishment device and multiple blockholders make that device *dynamically consistent* (a single blockholder's promise to bid price to value is not credible); combined blockholder intervention Σbi = φbβ(1/I) − 1 falls in I (free-rider).
- **Prop 4 (Firm-value optimum)**: **I* = (φa − φb)/φb** — increasing in managerial-effort productivity, decreasing in blockholder-intervention productivity. Prop 5 (social optimum) and Prop 6 (private optimum) are unique positive roots of displayed polynomials; same comparative statics in φa, φb.
- **Prop 7–8 (Costly information)**: with info cost c, only J = min{I, n} blockholders become informed, where n solves σησε/[√n(n+1)] = c; optimal I*_costly = min{(φa−φb)/φb, n}. **Now liquidity σε matters**: higher σε raises trading profits, raises n, raises I*, and raises firm value. This is the paper's channel by which liquidity strengthens governance-by-trading: it *supports a larger population of informed traders*, not larger trades.
- **Prop 9–10 (Complementarities)**: Leontief (positive complements) flips the φa/φb comparative statics; max-form (negative complements) yields corner I* ∈ {1, ∞}.
- **§4.3**: with manager weight ω on price, I* rises in ω (trading matters more when the manager cares about price).

**Functional forms that load results:** normal noise + normal fundamental noise (linear Kyle equilibrium); log production/linear cost (technology-neutral split); perfect information endowment in core model. The I/(I+1) informativeness formula is entirely a Kyle–Cournot artifact — clean but brittle to the information structure.

**Institutional facts:** Table 1 (Dlugosz et al. 2006 data, 1,240 U.S. firms, 2001, 5% threshold): 70% of firms have ≥2 blockholders, 26% have ≥4 (outside-only: 57%/17%); Holderness (2009) underlying data: 74%/26%; Europe 34–48% multiple blockholders at a 10% threshold (Laeven–Levine, Maury–Pajuste, Faccio–Lang). 13(d) 5% and 10% insider thresholds discussed as *causes* of fragmented structures. McCahery et al.: trading used by 80% of institutions, voting against management 66%. Empirical support cited: Gallagher, Gardner & Swan (2010) (more blockholders → lower trading profits, higher price efficiency, better subsequent performance — direct test of the mechanism); Gorton, Huang & Kang (2010); Boehmer & Kelley (2009); Bharath, Jayaraman & Nagar (2010) (liquidity more valuable with more blockholders).

**Referee-facing strengths:** first model with *both* governance mechanisms active for every blockholder; the commitment/dynamic-consistency argument (competition as commitment to reward effort) is deep and portable; closed-form optimum I* delivers refutable cross-sectional predictions. **Weaknesses:** (i) blockholders are exogenously informed in the core — the trading margin is costless information transmission, so "governance through trading" is close to assumed until §4.1; (ii) bi public is admitted tractability (trading and effort separable); (iii) Kyle neutrality means the headline liquidity comparative statics only appear in the extension; (iv) no bid-ask/price-impact microfoundation of σε; (v) symmetric blockholders, single round — no stake building, no disclosure, no exit-vs-voice choice *within* a blockholder (the choice is across the structure).

**Implications for repositioning our paper:**
- *Adopt the "which margin does liquidity strengthen?" taxonomy.* Edmans–Manso: liquidity strengthens the trading margin by sustaining informed entry (higher n). Ours: liquidity (κ) weakens order-flow inference but cheapens exit — the voice margin responds nonmonotonically. Position our κ comparative static as the missing cell: liquidity's effect on the *voice* margin when voice is disclosure-constrained.
- *The I/(I+1) formula is the elegance benchmark*: one fraction, one intuition (competition as commitment), everything else flows. Our equivalent should be a similarly compact object — e.g., a scalar function of κ and the disclosure threshold that signs dΔ/dκ by single-crossing.
- *Cite* for the multi-blockholder motivation (our single blockholder is a limitation a referee will note; E&M 2011 is the citation that multiple blockholders govern through trading competition — our disclosure margin is orthogonal to their number-of-blockholders margin).
- *Differentiate*: they hold β fixed and vary I; we hold the blockholder fixed and vary the information regime (disclosed vs inferred). Their liquidity is Kyle-neutral; ours is not, because our noise law (ternary) breaks the exact-offset property — worth stating as a feature: with discrete order flow, liquidity is never neutral for inference, which is exactly why disclosure policy matters.

## Paper 3: Back, Collin-Dufresne, Fos, Li & Ljungqvist (2018), "Activism, Strategic Trading, and Liquidity"

**Citation & access.** *Econometrica* 86(4), July 2018, 1431–1463. Local PDF `lit/Back-ACTIVISMSTRATEGICTRADING-2018.pdf` (JSTOR scan, 34 pp.), read in full including Theorems 1–2, the five examples, and the discussion; appendices (proofs, single-period model) skimmed for method.

**Research question.** How do market liquidity and activism affect each other when the activist's private information is her *own position* (hence her intentions), and she can affect firm value by costly effort? Bidirectional causality: liquidity affects stake accumulation and the activism decision; anticipated activism affects liquidity through adverse selection about intentions.

**Model architecture.** **Continuous-time Kyle (1985)/Back (1992) on [0, T]:**
- Strategic trader (potential activist) knows her initial stake X0 ~ N(μx, σx²) privately; noise trades Z = Brownian motion, instantaneous vol σ; market makers competitive/risk-neutral, price P(t, Yt) = E[V(XT) | order-flow history], Y = cumulative order flow.
- Activism technology is **general**: at T the activist with terminal stake x chooses value v to maximize vx − C(v); G(x) = sup_v{vx − C(v)} (convex envelope); optimal value V(x) = G'(x) a.e. — the marginal value of a share equals the value she will create. Cost C lower semicontinuous, superlinear at extremes (C = ∞ for infeasible v).
- Equilibrium: pair (P, θ) with P martingale-consistent and θ optimal; "no-doubling" admissibility. Continuous time chosen because discrete time is intractable here (Appendix D: single-period version only solves in uninteresting special cases; trading strategy there is *not* cost-function-independent).

**Key results (all analytic; figures illustrative):**
- **Theorem 1**: closed-form equilibrium for arbitrary C. Price P(t, y) = E[V(μx + ΛZT) | Zt = y]; trading strategy θ is **linear in X and Y and independent of the cost function C** (a striking modularity result — "the strategy can be fully specified without specifying the cost function"); order flow Y is a Brownian motion on the market's filtration (inconspicuous insider trading, via a *generalized Brownian bridge* on the activist's filtration); terminal stake XT = μx + ΛYT with Λ a closed-form constant (> 2) determined by (σx, σ, T); price equals marginal value at T: P(T, YT) = V(XT). Kyle's lambda λ(t, y) = ∂P/∂y is a martingale on the market's information. Because V(XT) rises in XT, the activist offsets noise **more than one-for-one** (coefficient Λ/(Λ−1) > 1 on −ZT) — buys when noise sells, sells when noise buys.
- **Theorem 2**: comparative statics hinge on **convexity/concavity of V** (a mean-preserving-spread/Jensen logic): (i) more noise trading σ raises efficiency iff V convex, lowers iff V concave; (ii) higher expected initial block μx always raises efficiency, and reduces (raises) liquidity if V convex (concave); (iii) more block uncertainty σx raises efficiency iff V convex; reduces liquidity under a regularity condition. No general sign for ∂λ/∂σ: more noise can **reduce market liquidity** (violating Treynor/Kyle intuition) because noise amplifies uncertainty about the activist's terminal position — adverse selection about *intentions*.
- **Five examples (Table I–III closed forms)**: (1) quadratic cost ⇒ V affine ⇒ efficiency independent of σ, σx; (2) asymmetric quadratic (no value destruction) ⇒ V convex ⇒ noise raises efficiency; liquidity and efficiency move together in σ, opposite in μx, σx, productivity; (3) exponential ⇒ V = v0e^(ψx) convex, price GBM, constant percentage price impact; (4) **binary** (Maug 1998's technology; step-function V): noise raises efficiency iff Aμx < c (small expected toehold — stake must be built) — Maug's threshold result recovered; liquidity effects switch sign with |μx − c/A|; (5) probabilistic binary (bounded V; success probability 1 − e^(−Ax/c)).
- **Headline departures from prior literature**: (a) the liquidity–efficiency relation is **independent of the initial stake for a broad set of (non-binary) activism technologies** — Maug's stake-threshold gate is a binary-technology artifact; (b) noise trading can reduce market liquidity; (c) causality also runs activism → liquidity.

**Functional forms that load results:** normal prior on X0 + Brownian noise (filtering closed forms); risk neutrality; competitive market makers; single activist; fixed disclosure date T. The *point* of the paper is that which side of the binary/continuous divide the technology C falls on determines the signs — they make functional form the headline variable.

**Institutional facts:** Schedule 13D: 5% threshold, with a pre-disclosure window in which the activist trades anonymously; Section 13(f): quarterly long-position disclosure for managers > $100M AUM (no shorts); Collin-Dufresne & Fos (2015a): average activist holds **7.51%** at first 13D (8.70% with derivatives, Collin-Dufresne–Fos–Muravyev) — so costs are recouped from a small toehold, justifying the per-share efficiency measure; Bebchuk, Brav, Jackson & Jiang (2013) debate on shortening the 13D window — the paper maps window length T to cumulative noise σ²T ("reducing T is isomorphic to reducing σ"); The Economist (2015): half of S&P 500 had an activist 2010–2014, one in seven targeted; Brav et al. (2008) classification of campaign goals as the empirical handle on activism-technology heterogeneity; Norges Bank (9,000+ firms) and CalPERS as trading-plus-engagement institutions.

**Referee-facing strengths:** maximal generality with closed forms — the "trading strategy independent of the cost function" result is genuinely elegant; reverses a literature's causality assumption; delivers a clean reconciliation (technology curvature) of the Coffee/Bhide vs. Maug debate. **Weaknesses:** (i) continuous-time machinery (generalized Brownian bridge, no-doubling conditions) is inaccessible to much of the JF/RFS audience — a referee can love the theorem and still demand a discrete-time robustness check the authors only conjecture; (ii) efficiency measured as price level, not value net of activism cost — welfare statements are incomplete; (iii) X0 normal ⇒ negative stakes possible; (iv) activism technology common knowledge, effort fully observed at T — no moral hazard in the *observability* dimension, only in the stake dimension; (v) comparative statics are sign-results conditional on V's curvature, which is not directly observable — empiricists must proxy technology type via campaign goals.

**Implications for repositioning our paper:**
- *This is the strongest "liquidity–voice ambiguity is technology-dependent" citation.* Their message — sign of d(efficiency)/d(liquidity) depends on the curvature of the value-from-stake function V(x) — legitimizes our hump: nonmonotonicity in liquidity is the rule, not the exception, once position constraints bind. Cite Theorem 2(i) alongside Edmans (2009) Prop 3 as the two anchors for R1's plausibility.
- *Their disclosure mapping is directly usable*: 13D window length T ≡ cumulative camouflage σ²T. Our disclosure-threshold variation (5%-style trigger, strict vs lenient jurisdictions) is the natural cross-sectional counterpart; cite their §7 discussion to frame R2's policy relevance (window-shortening debate).
- *Differentiate sharply*: in BCFLL the activist has no exogenous private information about firm value — only about her intentions; she cannot choose *quiet* voice (action is observed at T) and cannot *exit* as a governance signal. Our blockholder's information advantage about v, the Exit/Hold/Quiet/Public menu, and the bidder-feedback channel are all absent here. Our model is complementary: they study stake→value; we study signal→mode-choice→bid.
- *Elegance lesson*: one general theorem + a taxonomy of technologies, with each comparative static *signed by a curvature condition on primitives*. Our analog: sign dΔ^min/dκ by a curvature/elasticity condition (the draft already gestures at "price-elasticity of bids" — make that the *headline condition*, as BCFLL make convexity of V theirs).
- *Do not imitate*: continuous time would price out our audience and destroy the discrete order-flow/disclosure-flag structure that generates the disclosed/inferred split.

## Paper 4: Levit, Malenko & Maug (2024), "Trading and Shareholder Democracy"

**Citation & access.** Levit, Doron, Nadya Malenko, and Ernst Maug, 2024, *Journal of Finance* 79(1), 257–304. Accessed as ECGI Finance WP 631/2019 (March 2024 version, JF-formatted, identical pagination to the published article): https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/tradingandshareholderdemocracy.pdf. Read in full (60 pp. main text + proofs); Internet Appendix extensions (index investors, social concerns, post-vote trading) read as summarized in Section V of the main text.

**Research question.** Does shareholder voting aggregate preferences efficiently when the shareholder base forms endogenously through trading before the vote? No: trading aligns the voter base with the *expected* outcome (self-fulfilling multiple equilibria); prices (marginal shareholder) and welfare (average shareholder) diverge and can move in opposite directions; relaxing trading frictions can *reduce* welfare by empowering extremists; delegation to a suitably biased board can dominate "shareholder democracy."

**Model architecture.** Deliberately *not* Kyle:
- Continuum (measure 1) of risk-neutral shareholders, each endowed with ē shares; one proposal vs. baseline; common-value state θ ∈ {−1, +1} (proposal good iff θ = +1) plus private bias b ~ G (differentiable cdf, full support). Valuation v(d, θ, b) = v0 + (θ + b)(d − δ), δ ∈ [0,1] the disagreement share; voting rule: approve iff fraction > τ; vote is sincere/pivotal-style (undominated): support iff b + q > 0, where q = E[θ | public signal], public signal observed *after* trading, before voting. **No asymmetric information, no noise traders, no market maker.**
- Trading: competitive market, price p clears; each shareholder may buy up to x̄ or sell up to ē (no short sales). **Market depth δ ≡ x̄/(x̄ + ē)** is *the* liquidity/trading-friction parameter — a position limit, not a noise intensity.
- Equilibrium: subgame-perfect Nash in undominated strategies; cutoff approval rules (Lemma 1).

**Key results (all analytic, quantile closed forms):**
- **Prop 1 (vote, no trade)**: unique equilibrium, approval cutoff qNoTrade = −G⁻¹(1−τ) (median voter logic).
- **Prop 2 (trade, no vote; board decides with rule q*)**: unique equilibrium; if H(q*) > δ the equilibrium is "activist" (high-b types buy, low-b sell) with marginal shareholder **ba = G⁻¹(δ)**; if H(q*) < δ, "conservative" with **bc = G⁻¹(1−δ)**; price = marginal shareholder's valuation. Trading = matching between shareholders and firms.
- **Prop 3 (trade + vote)**: activist equilibrium exists iff H(qa) > δ with cutoff **qa = −G⁻¹(1−τ(1−δ))**; conservative exists iff H(qc) < δ with **qc = −G⁻¹((1−τ)(1−δ))**; equilibrium always exists; both can exist simultaneously — **self-fulfilling multiplicity from the trading–voting feedback loop**.
- **Corollaries 1–2**: marginal shareholder *and* median voter become more extreme as depth δ rises; as δ → 1 the firm is held by a single extreme type.
- **Lemma 2**: welfare of initial shareholders = valuation of the **average post-trade shareholder** (linearity of v in b + market clearing) — while price = valuation of the **marginal** shareholder and the decision = will of the **median voter**. The entire paper is the geometry of these three points.
- **Prop 4**: removing trading frictions (δ → 1) dominates any fricted equilibrium of the same type (all three identities merge).
- **Prop 5**: multiplicity requires sufficiently high depth, intermediate τ, sufficient preference heterogeneity, and intermediate δ-sensitivity of valuations.
- **Lemma 3 / Prop 6 / Corollary 3**: price maximized when median voter = marginal shareholder; welfare maximized when median voter = average shareholder; if the median voter lies between them, any parameter moving the median (e.g., majority requirement τ) moves price and welfare **in opposite directions** — event studies of votes can get the wrong *sign* for welfare.
- **Prop 7 (headline liquidity result)**: if the no-trade median voter is more extreme than the average shareholder and |H(qNoTrade) − δ| is small, there exists δ* > 0 such that **welfare decreases in depth for δ < δ*** — relaxing trading frictions helps extremists accumulate voting weight; the gain from trade is outweighed by the aggravated voting friction. (With a board instead of a vote — Lemma 4 — more depth always helps: the result is about the *interaction* of trading and voting frictions.)
- **Prop 8**: optimal board is biased toward the average *post-trade* shareholder (b*m = β̄a or β̄c ≠ E[b]); delegation strictly beats voting except in a knife-edge case — and shareholders may collectively fail to delegate (internet appendix).
- **Extensions**: index investors (don't trade, do vote) moderate the median voter, restore uniqueness for large index share, and have a nonmonotone price effect; social concerns (E&S utility beyond holdings) amplify biases, make multiplicity more likely, can lower price; post-vote trading preserves all results and generates opposite-signed price vs welfare reactions to vote outcomes (stronger when the vote is close).

**Functional forms that load results:** linearity of v in b (so welfare = average-shareholder valuation); the (x̄, ē) position limits (market clearing yields the quantile formulas); binary state + scalar public signal q. Everything else is distribution-free in G — the propositions are statements about quantiles of G, which is why the paper is so robust across applications (horizons, taxes, private benefits, E&S, beliefs).

**Institutional facts:** record-date mechanics (record date after trading; proxy-advisor recommendations ~1 month after record date — Li, Maug & Schwartz-Ziv 2022); simple vs supermajority heterogeneity across firms; nonbinding proposals effectively binding with endogenous threshold (Levit & Malenko 2011); Big-3 index funds cast ~25% of S&P 500 votes (Bebchuk & Hirst 2019); ~30% of recent shareholder proposals are E&S (Bolton et al. 2020; Bubb & Catan 2022); Cox, Mondino & Thomas (2019) — M&A targets experience large pre-vote ownership turnover predicting approval; Exxon/Engine No. 1 proxy fight (WSJ 2021); preference factor-structure evidence (one or two factors explain voting heterogeneity).

**Referee-facing strengths:** a genuinely different trading technology (Walrasian with position limits — no noise traders needed) that makes the *composition* of the shareholder base, rather than price informativeness, the object of study; the marginal/median/average trichotomy is a durable conceptual contribution; immediate warnings for event-study empirics. **Weaknesses:** (i) no asymmetric information at all — prices play no information-aggregation role, so the model is silent on the very channel (inference from order flow) that drives our paper; (ii) the tie-breaking rule (no trade when indifferent) does real work in killing knife-edge equilibria; (iii) welfare = average initial shareholder is a veil-of-ignorance choice, contestable; (iv) multiplicity means empirical predictions are set-valued; (v) one proposal, one dimension, simultaneous trading — no dynamics of stake building.

**Implications for repositioning our paper:**
- *Trading-technology contrast to exploit*: LMM show how far one can go with a **Walrasian market + position limits and no noise traders at all** — liquidity = depth δ, and its welfare effect runs through *who ends up holding the shares*. Our paper keeps the Kyle-style inference channel but should cite LMM as proof that top-3 journals now accept non-Kyle trading technologies in governance models; the discrete order-flow choice (à la Edmans–Goldstein–Jiang) is a *middle* point between Kyle-continuous and LMM-Walrasian and should be defended as such.
- *Their Prop 7 is the voting-side analog of our hump*: more trading can hurt because it reshapes the decision-relevant shareholder. Ours: more noise reshapes the *inference* environment. Both say "liquidity is not governance-neutral." Use LMM 2024 to frame the broad theme; differentiate on channel (preference composition vs. information composition).
- *The marginal vs median vs average decomposition* suggests a presentational device for our paper: our disclosure flag similarly creates two "regimes" (disclosed/inferred) with different effective price-setters; an analogous three-object decomposition (bidder's required premium, market maker's posterior, blockholder's payoff) could make R1–R2 transparent.
- *Cite for empirics anchor*: pre-vote turnover predicts voting outcomes (Cox et al. 2019; Li, Maug & Schwartz-Ziv 2022) — directly supports taking our model's turnover/disclosure predictions to 13D + voting data.

## Cross-cutting synthesis

### A. Trading technologies: Kyle vs discrete order flow (the strand's core comparison)

| Paper | Trading technology | Noise/liquidity parameter | Where tractability comes from | What liquidity does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Edmans 2009 | Static Kyle, one round, MM sets P = E[V\|d, s] | ν via u ~ exp(λ), λ = 1/(ν(1−α)) | **Exponential noise + binary V** ⇒ closed-form sale β = min(1/λ, α), detection prob e^(−λβ) | Hump in ν with fixed α (camouflage vs enabling); monotone ↑ if α endogenous |
| Edmans & Manso 2011 | Static multi-insider Kyle (Cournot) | σε (normal noise vol) | Normal/normal linear equilibrium ⇒ informativeness I/(I+1) | Neutral in core (Kyle neutrality); ↑ via informed-entry margin n under costly info |
| Back et al. 2018 | Continuous-time Kyle, Brownian noise | σ (noise vol), σx (stake uncertainty), T | Generalized Brownian bridge; strategy linear & cost-independent; signs via **convexity of V** | Ambiguous by technology: ↑ efficiency iff V convex; can *reduce* market liquidity |
| LMM 2024 | **Walrasian, position limits, no noise traders, no MM** | Depth δ = x̄/(x̄+ē) | Quantile closed forms in G; linear-in-b valuations | ↑δ ⇒ more extreme shareholder base; multiplicity ↑; welfare can fall (Prop 7) |
| **Our paper** | Static discrete order flow (ternary z), MM prices from X and disclosure flag D, bidder entry | κ = P(z = ±1) | Ternary noise ⇒ p0 = 1−κ, p1 = κ/2 posteriors; fixed point over cutoffs | Hump in κ (R1, numeric); disclosure attenuates κ-sensitivity (R2) |

**Lessons:** (1) Kyle-continuous buys closed forms and clean Cournot/curvature comparative statics but forces liquidity neutrality or technology-dependence; (2) discrete order flow (Edmans 2009's exponential; our ternary; EGJ 2012/2015's framework) is where *inference regimes* (detected vs not; disclosed vs inferred) live — that is precisely our contribution's habitat; (3) LMM prove a top-3 journal will take a non-Kyle, no-noise-trader technology if the economic object (shareholder-base composition) is crisp. The defensible positioning: *discrete order flow is the minimal technology in which a disclosure flag can partition inference* — continuous Kyle would blur the very distinction our paper exploits.

### B. What liquidity does to voice — the accumulated answer

- Edmans 2009: with position limits, liquidity hump — enables then camouflages (Prop 3); with endogenous stakes, monotone positive (Prop 4). **Voice substitute = retention/loyalty signal.**
- Edmans & Manso 2011: liquidity supports a larger informed-trader population (costly info extension); governance-through-trading strengthened; voice (intervention) weakened by numbers, not by liquidity per se.
- Back et al. 2018: sign of liquidity → activism depends on activism-technology curvature; noise can destroy market liquidity itself; stake-threshold gates are binary-technology artifacts.
- LMM 2024: depth (liquidity) hands voting weight to extremists; welfare effect of "more market" can be negative; index (non-trading) investors moderate.
- **Synthesis for our paper**: across this class, liquidity is never governance-neutral, and its effect on voice is mediated by *which margin binds*: position limits (Edmans), entry into information (E&M), technology curvature (BCFLL), preference composition (LMM), and in ours **inference composition** (disclosed vs inferred). Our R1 hump is the inference-margin hump; R2 (disclosure attenuation) is the new institutional margin. This taxonomy is itself a framing device for the introduction.

### C. What a simple-yet-elegant core looks like (design patterns to copy)

1. **One scalar statistic carries the economics**: E&M's I/(I+1); Edmans's e^(−λβ); BCFLL's V-convexity; LMM's (marginal, median, average) triple. Our candidate: a scalar "inferred-premium share" (fraction of the activism premium transmitted through the nondisclosed branch) as a closed-form function of κ and the disclosure threshold — if R1/R2 can be restated as comparative statics of that scalar, the paper becomes provable.
2. **Functional forms chosen so indifference conditions are closed form**: exponential noise, log production, ternary noise, quantile cutoffs. Our ternary z already fits; the weak link is the *bidder-entry* layer (normal synergy draw ⇒ Φ-based bid probability) which forces the Brouwer fixed point. An elegant simplification would make bid incidence a logit/linear function of price, or make the bidder's rule a cutoff in P, restoring closed-form cutoff recursion.
3. **Prove comparative statics by curvature/single-crossing conditions on primitives, not by fixed-point numerics**: BCFLL Theorem 2 is the template (mean-preserving spread + convexity); LMM Prop 7 (threshold condition on |H(qNoTrade) − δ|) is the template for a *signed region* result. Our R1 could be restated as: "Δ^min is single-peaked whenever [elasticity condition on p(P)]" — the draft's 'price-elasticity of bids' condition should be promoted from fine print to the headline assumption, exactly as BCFLL headline V-convexity.
4. **Honest uniqueness practice**: E&M and LMM get uniqueness by construction (linear Kyle; tie-breaking + cutoff rules); BCFLL by fixed-point with verification; EGJ 2015 (our antecedent) do exactly what we do (existence + numerical uniqueness) and published in AER — so numerical uniqueness is *not* disqualifying, but the three comparison papers show the payoff to killing the fixed point.
5. **Institutional primitives as parameters**: 13D 5%/10% thresholds (Edmans, E&M, BCFLL), 13D window length T (BCFLL), majority requirement τ and record date (LMM). Our disclosure threshold θ (5% → lower, strict jurisdictions) belongs in this list — R2 is squarely in the genre and is our most institutional, hence most repositionable, result.

### D. Most decision-relevant findings for repositioning (summary for the orchestrator)

1. **Our hump has pedigrees**: Edmans (2009) Prop 3 (liquidity hump under position limits) and BCFLL (2018) Thm 2 (technology-dependent sign) jointly establish that nonmonotone liquidity effects are the expected case in this literature — cite both and stop being defensive about R1; instead spend the effort on a *primitive condition* that signs it (BCFLL's convexity template).
2. **The fixed point is optional, not intrinsic**: all four papers characterize equilibrium in closed form via a chosen noise/market technology; the two layers of our model that force Brouwer (bidder entry + cross-cutoff feedback) should be simplified toward cutoff rules, following LMM's quantile-characterization style.
3. **Disclosure-threshold variation is our unique institutional margin**: BCFLL's T↔σ²T mapping and the 13D-window debate (Bebchuk et al. 2013) + LMM's majority-requirement comparative statics show referees reward institutional-parameter comparative statics; R2 (disclosure attenuation) should lead the repositioned paper, with R1 as supporting evidence.
4. **Non-Kyle trading technologies are now top-3-acceptable** (LMM 2024, JF): our discrete order-flow + disclosure-flag structure should be *advertised* as the minimal technology supporting regime-partitioned inference, not apologized for.
5. **Differentiation line**: in Edmans 2009/E&M 2011/BCFLL 2018 the blockholder's information is about value or intentions, but *voice intensity and its observability are never chosen*; LMM 2024 endogenize the voter base but remove information asymmetry entirely. Our cell — endogenous voice mode with stake-triggered observability and price-feedback into takeover bids — is empty in all four. That is the repositioning white space.
