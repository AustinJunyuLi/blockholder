## 3. Critical Issues (Must-Fix)

### 3.1 Prop 6 — the headline assumes its own conclusion (referee-fatal)

**The issue.** Line 554: *"Assume the deterrence effect at $\kappa\to0$ is sufficiently strong … such that $\Delta^{\min}(\tilde\kappa) > \lim_{\kappa\to0}\Delta^{\min}(\kappa)$."* That inequality **is** the non-monotonicity to be proved. The appendix "proof" (line ~1252) applies Weierstrass to relabel the assumed inequality a "verifiable sufficient condition." A result that is assumed is not a contribution. Compounding this, the supporting **Endpoints Lemma is factually wrong** (lines 564, 1243): it claims voice collapses as $\kappa\to1$ and that posteriors "converge to the unconditional prior across all nondisclosed states." Both are false — the $k_0$ indifference RHS is bounded below by $\delta\cdot\min(\tilde m - m_0,\ \tilde\Delta)>0$ with $C(\infty)=0$, so voice *persists*; and the $\kappa=1$ posteriors are the two-point law $\{0,\bar\pi\}$, not the prior.

**Why a top-journal referee cares.** *Petitio principii* in the paper's single most-promoted theorem is an instant credibility hit; a referee who catches it (it is obvious) will reread every other proof adversarially. Worse, even a *perfectly proved* "nonmonotone liquidity" headline is partly pre-empted by Back et al. (2018), who already proved the liquidity-efficiency relation is sign-ambiguous depending on technology. So the unproved version is fatal and the naive proved version is merely incremental.

**The concrete fix — integrate the rigor memo's corrected proof, WITH three corrections the memo itself gets wrong.** The memo's architecture is **substantially correct at baseline** (verified numerically against the paper's own price fixed point): endpoint symmetry is *exact*; $\mathbb{E}[\pi\mid D{=}0]$ is $\kappa$-invariant while $\mathrm{Var}[\pi\mid D{=}0]$ is robustly U-shaped (interior min at $\kappa\approx0.48$–$0.58$); and at baseline the full $\Delta^{\min}$ (not just $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}$) is a genuine hump (interior max $\kappa\approx0.55$, equal endpoints, amplitude $\approx 1.9\%$ of level). Implement it, but fix:

- **Correction (a): the sufficient condition certifies the WRONG function.** The memo derives concavity condition $(C^*):\ \bar m(\pi)\,T(\pi) < 2\sigma_\xi$ for $g(\pi)=\bar m(\pi)\,p(\pi)$. But the object that actually enters $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}$ (Definition, line 537) is $(\tilde m - m_0)\,\mathbb{E}[\pi\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\text{bid}\}]$, whose integrand is $h(\pi)=\pi\,p(\pi)$. These differ by the affine factor $\bar m(\pi)$. At baseline *both* happen to be concave ($\max g''=-0.058$, $\max h''=-0.415$), so the conclusion survives — but by luck of curvature, not by logic. **Re-derive the concavity condition for $h(\pi)=\pi p(\pi)$:** $h''=2p'(\pi)+\pi p''(\pi)$ with full feedback $T'=[(\tilde m - m_0)+P'(\pi)]/\sigma_\xi$. Because the realized $D=0$ support is the two-point set $\{0,\bar\pi\}$, you only need $h$ concave **on the chord over $[0,\bar\pi]$**, not globally. State the resulting condition $(C^{**})$ and report the baseline margin.

- **Correction (b): the GE cutoff-shift channel is a genuine, unsigned, first-order term — the envelope theorem does NOT rescue it.** The full derivative is
  $$\frac{d\Delta^{\min}}{d\kappa} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \Delta^{\min}}{\partial \kappa}\Big|_{\text{cutoffs}}}_{\text{PE term (memo handles this)}} + \sum_i \frac{\partial \Delta^{\min}}{\partial k_i}\,\frac{dk_i}{d\kappa}.$$
  $\Delta^{\min}$ is *minority welfare*, **not the blockholder's own objective**, so no first-order condition zeroes the second sum. "PE-analytic + numerical GE caveat" is not acceptable for a headline. **Fix:** prove $\kappa\mapsto(k_1,k_0,k_D)$ is $C^1$ via the implicit function theorem on the cutoff map $\mathbf{T}$ at interior $\kappa$ (isolating the finitely many $\kappa$ where a cutoff crosses an order-flow boundary $\{k_i=\mu+j\sigma_s\}$), then **bound** $\sum_i(\partial\Delta^{\min}/\partial k_i)(dk_i/d\kappa)$ explicitly and show it does not overturn the PE hump near the interior peak. Do not invoke the envelope theorem.

- **Correction (c): $(C^*)$/$(C^{**})$ must be a STATED HYPOTHESIS — the hump is NOT unconditional.** Verified mapping: violate the condition and the full $\Delta^{\min}$ *flips from hump to trough*. Baseline $(C^*)$ margin $-0.637$ → hump (amplitude $1.87\%$); $\sigma_\xi=0.25$ margin $-0.180$ → still hump ($3.30\%$); but $\sigma_\xi=0.10$ (margin $+1.18$) → **trough**; $\bar S=0.60$ (margin $+2.58$) → **trough**. So the condition is *non-vacuous and correctly oriented*: bids must be sufficiently *price-elastic* (synergy dispersion $\sigma_\xi$ large relative to the premium wedge) for the inference channel to dominate. The current Prop 6 states the hump unconditionally; that claim is **false**. Add the condition to the standing assumptions / proposition hypotheses and add a robustness table exhibiting the hump→trough transition.

- **Two remaining analytic gaps (do not leave numerical):** (i) prove $\mathrm{Var}[\pi\mid D{=}0](\kappa)$ U-shaped *in closed form* (with $x=\kappa/2$, $y=1-2x$, write Var as a rational function of $\kappa$, show derivative negative-then-positive with a unique interior zero); (ii) prove *strict quasiconcavity* of $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}$ on $(0,1)$ to upgrade "an interior extremum exists" to "single-peaked hump" and kill the W-shape caveat (line 562). Replacing one numerically-asserted claim (the old hump) with another (U-shaped variance) is not progress for a JF/JFE referee.

- **Numerical hygiene:** the baseline hump (~1.9%) sits uncomfortably near the solver's `TOL_RESIDUAL = 5e-3` (params.py line 29) and the $\kappa$-sweep uses a single warm-start (`compute_series_over_kappa`), not the multi-start `solve_valid`. Tighten to $\le 10^{-5}$ for the $\Delta^{\min}$ sweep, switch to multi-start, and report $\Delta^{\min}$ at both true endpoints ($\kappa=10^{-6}$, $1-10^{-6}$) to exhibit the exact symmetry.

**Does this fully close the gap?** **Partly.** The memo's fix *does* convert Prop 6 from circular to a genuine sufficient-condition theorem and *survives at baseline*. But it is **not yet referee-proof** until: the condition is restated for the correct integrand $h=\pi p$ (a), the GE cutoff-shift channel is signed/bounded analytically (b), the condition is promoted to a stated hypothesis (c), and the variance U-shape and single-peakedness are proved analytically. The economic reframing is mandatory too: see the next item.

**Reframe the economics (a subtlety the memo misses).** Endpoint symmetry *strengthens rigor but weakens the directional policy story.* $\Delta^{\min}(0)=\Delta^{\min}(1)$ means the no-liquidity and full-liquidity worlds deliver minorities the *identical* premium via opposite mechanisms (perfect revelation vs zero revelation). This **refutes** the draft's directional narrative ("extreme liquidity destroys engagement, so $\Delta^{\min}$ is minimized at high $\kappa$"). Reframe the contribution around the **interior-optimal liquidity $\kappa^\dagger$** and $(C^{**})$ as the condition that makes it a *maximum* (vs a minimum when the condition fails), tied to the disclosure threshold $\tau$. Policy message becomes "the premium-maximizing liquidity depends on the 13D threshold" (which symmetry supports) rather than "more liquidity hurts minorities" (which symmetry refutes). This also cleanly differentiates from Back et al.'s technology-driven sign-ambiguity.

### 3.2 Existence is incomplete; uniqueness is numerical-only (mislabeled as a Proposition)

**The issue (existence).** Brouwer requires $\mathbf{T}$ to map the compact convex domain $\Theta$ *into itself*; the self-map property is asserted by hand ("we can choose $\underline k,\bar k$ large enough … such that best-response cutoffs strictly map into the interior of $\Theta$," line 1221). $\mathbf{T}$ could map an interior point to a vector that *violates the ordering* $k_1\le k_0\le k_D$, exiting $\Theta$. Continuity (Step 3, line 951) is merely asserted "by construction." The crucial **$B_P - B_Q > 0$ step (line 942)** — which delivers $k_D$ and the whole four-region ordering — is **not proved**; the text concedes the algebraic relation $h_P=2>1=h_Q$ "does not guarantee $2(1-p_P)>1-p_Q$" and substitutes a narrative ("$p(X,1)$ sufficiently lower than $p(X,0)$").

**The issue (uniqueness).** A6 (contraction) is *assumed*, then "confirmed" by observing iteration convergence — but convergence of damped iteration is neither necessary nor sufficient for a spectral radius $<1$. The paper itself calls the bound "intractable." A "Proposition (Uniqueness)" that is actually a numerical observation will be rejected as mislabeled, and multiplicity directly threatens the headline: if equilibrium is non-unique, $\kappa\mapsto(k_1,k_0,k_D)$ is a *correspondence*, and the single-warm-start sweep may be jumping branches — "the" hump could be a branch-selection artifact.

**Why a referee cares.** In the EGJ/Back-et-al tradition, an unverified self-map + unverified continuity + an asserted single-crossing step + numerical-only uniqueness is not an acceptable equilibrium section at top-10. (EGJ used numerical uniqueness, but on a *far simpler* map without a coupled three-cutoff fixed point with price feedback.)

**The concrete fix.**
- **Self-map:** show each best-response cutoff is bounded (the payoff difference crosses zero at finite $s$ given A1's growth conditions); prove **ordering preservation** by establishing $B_H>0$ and $B_P-B_Q>0$ *globally on $\Theta$*; handle the collapse faces $k_1=k_0$, $k_0=k_D$ (prove strict interiority under A1 + $\chi>0$, or extend $\mathbf{T}$ continuously to the closed faces — note $\Theta$ = box $\cap$ two half-spaces is a convex polytope, so the ordering constraints *are* the half-spaces; state this).
- **$B_P>B_Q$ as a Lemma:** $B_P-B_Q=\delta[1+\mathbb{E}_z p(X,0) - 2\mathbb{E}_z p(X,1)]$, positive if $p(X,1)\le 1/2$ for all $X$, i.e. the disclosed bid threshold $T(X,1)\ge 0$, i.e. $\bar S - K - P^*(X,1)\le \tilde m$. State this as a clean condition on synergy net of the fully-priced engaged firm, prove it follows from the interior-bid bounds (or add as a standing assumption with economic justification: disclosed engaged firms are bid for less than half the time), and **verify at the calibration.**
- **Uniqueness — pick one:** (a) *derive* the contraction: bound $\|D\mathbf{T}\|_\infty \le \delta\sup|dp/dP|\cdot \mathrm{Lip}(\pi\text{ in }\omega)\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(\omega\text{ in }k)$ and give a primitive sufficient inequality (A5 bounds the feedback; $\sigma_e$ not too small bounds $d\pi/dk$) — this converts A6 from assumption to theorem; OR (b) prove uniqueness via monotone comparative statics / single-crossing on the scalar reduced map; OR (c) **honestly relabel**: state existence as the theorem, present uniqueness as "Numerical Regularity" (not a Proposition), and prove the comparative statics are robust *across all equilibria* (show the hump holds at every fixed point, or that the selected equilibrium is the unique stable one). Option (c) is the cheapest honest move for Path A.
- **A5 actually controls less than claimed:** the price-map derivative is $\delta[p^* + (P+\bar m - \hat V)p'(P)]$, *not* $\delta|dp/dP|$. Split A5 into A5a (inner price uniqueness, accounting for *both* terms: e.g. $\delta\bar p + \delta\,\tilde m/(\sigma_\xi\sqrt{2\pi})<1$) and A5b (cross-cutoff feedback). Baseline $\delta/\sigma_\xi=2.375$ vs bound $\approx 2.507$ leaves only a 5.3% margin — report it and add a $\sigma_\xi$ sensitivity table so the welfare/extension sweeps don't silently violate it.
- **A7 (price injectivity) is circular — drop it.** It assumes a property of the *equilibrium* price schedule before the equilibrium is constructed, and it can fail ($\pi(-2,0)=0$ and at high $\kappa$, $\pi(0,0)\to 0$). **Cleaner fix:** let the bidder condition on the publicly observed *price* $P$ directly, defining $p(P,D)$, $\bar m(P,D)$ on the price domain. Then injectivity is unnecessary (pooling $X$'s that share a price is harmless), and A7 is **removed entirely.** Recommended.
- **Monotone-cutoff restriction needs a real lemma, not "self-confirming."** Because full-support noise makes *all* info sets on-path (line 321), you **cannot** prune nonmonotone equilibria with belief refinements — this is a *double-edged* feature the draft mis-frames as a simplification. Prove a **global single-crossing / monotone-best-response lemma:** for *any* Bayes-consistent belief system $\pi(X,D)$, the payoff $U(q,a\mid s)=A+B\hat v(s)-aC(s)$ is monotone in $s$ because $\hat v(s)$ is increasing, $C(s)$ decreasing (A2), and the slopes are *totally ordered* ($B_E<B_H=B_Q<B_P$). The upper envelope of affine-minus-cost functions is then automatically a monotone partition — this kills nonmonotone equilibria for *all* beliefs. This is the rigorous replacement for the hand-wave.

### 3.3 Prop 8 and the entire welfare section are rhetorical, not analytical

**The issue.** The words "planner," "first-order condition," "FOC," "implicit function," "Jacobian," "envelope," and "argmax" appear **nowhere** in the 1432-line draft. The welfare section (lines 717–730) writes a total-surplus expectation (eq. 725) and then *asserts* every normative conclusion: $W(\kappa)$ hump-shaped, $\kappa^*\ne\kappa^\dagger$, "liquidity regulation operates implicitly as governance policy" — with **zero optimization**. $\kappa^*$ (line 730) is named but never characterized; the only optimizing object is the (circular) $\max$ over $\kappa$ *inside* Prop 6. Prop 8 (lines 783–790) has **no proof anywhere** (the proof list ends at Prop 6), is written with a `\approx` *inside a Proposition*, and is footnoted as "an intuitive heuristic." $\mathrm{sign}(\kappa^*-\kappa^\dagger)$ is asserted via a *tautology* (line 730 restates the definition of $W$ vs $W_{\min}$, not a comparative static). $W_{\mathrm{bid}}(\kappa)$ monotonicity is asserted though $\kappa$ moves *both* $P$ and the priced premium $\bar m$ (Prop 3), so the sign is genuinely non-obvious.

**Why a referee cares.** A normative claim about optimal liquidity with no objective and no optimality condition is an opinion backed by one simulated curve. Calling a `\approx` heuristic a "Proposition" tells the referee the theorem environment cannot be trusted, contaminating the genuine proofs. And the welfare hump *inherits* the Prop 6 / wrong-endpoint defect.

**The concrete fix.**
- **State the planner program:** $\kappa^*\in\arg\max_{\kappa\in(0,1)} W(\kappa)$ (eq. 725), $\kappa^\dagger\in\arg\max_\kappa \Delta^{\min}(\kappa)$. Derive the **FOC** $W'(\kappa)=0$ via the envelope theorem on the blockholder's privately-optimal cutoffs (so $dW/d\kappa$ picks up the direct effect on bid incidence and inference *plus* the non-internalized engagement externality). State the second-order/interiority condition.
- **Sign $\kappa^*-\kappa^\dagger$:** compute $W'(\kappa)-\Delta^{\min}{}'(\kappa) = dW_B/d\kappa + dW_{\mathrm{bid}}/d\kappa$ (the components the minority optimum ignores); show $>0$ at $\kappa^\dagger$ under an explicit primitive inequality, yielding $\kappa^*>\kappa^\dagger$ by single-peakedness. Commit to the sign.
- **Convert Prop 8 to a genuine comparative static** via IFT on the coupled cutoff fixed point $\mathbf{T}(k_1,k_0,k_D;\tau)=0$: (i) $\partial\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}/\partial\tau>0$ at fixed cutoffs (from Prop 2/3 monotonicity in $\omega_P$); (ii) IFT for $dk_0/d\tau$, $dk_D/d\tau$, hence $d(\omega_Q+\omega_P)/d\tau<0$ (deterrence); (iii) write the total derivative with `=` not `\approx` and state the primitive condition under which each sign dominates. Add a real appendix proof. If you will not do the IFT, **downgrade Prop 8 to a clearly-labeled "Heuristic Decomposition," not a Proposition.**
- **Engage the welfare benchmarks** the section currently ignores: **Levit-Malenko-Maug (2024, JF)** already prove more liquidity can *reduce* welfare and that prices are an invalid proxy for shareholder welfare — directly overlapping both the "liquidity not maximally good" claim *and* the choice to equate minority welfare with a price object ($\Delta^{\min}$ = takeover premium). Distinguish this paper's mechanism (stake-triggered-disclosure inference feeding bidder entry) from LMM's voting/clientele channel, and **defend $W_{\min}$ = takeover premium explicitly** via the Grossman-Hart free-rider argument (state it as a welfare argument, since LMM caution against price-as-welfare).
- **Numeraire check (eq. 725):** the "transfers cancel" justification is asserted without checking the timing inconsistency the code flags (EXIT proceeds undiscounted; HOLD/QV/PV terminal discounted by $\delta$). Either set $\delta=1$ in the welfare accounting (present-value units, as the line-280 footnote permits) and state it, or carry the $\delta$ terms and prove the residual nets to zero. Otherwise eq. (725) may be the wrong social objective.

### 3.4 Micro-foundations imposed by fiat (the part a top-10 referee attacks first)

**(i) $m_0, m_1$ are free primitives — the activism-premium channel is assumed, not derived.** The wedge $m_1-m_0$ drives all of $\Delta^{\mathrm{act}}=(\tilde m - m_0)\mathbb{E}[\pi\mathbf{1}\{\text{bid}\}]$ (Prop 3) and the headline hump; A3 ($\tilde m>m_0$) merely *assumes* the sign of the channel, so the paper *cannot fail* to find an activism premium. The distinguishing contribution over EGJ2015 (the takeover-premium channel) thus rests on a posited number. **Fix:** micro-found via **Nash bargaining over realized takeover surplus $(\bar S+\xi)$** between bidder and the engaged blockholder/board, with the blockholder's outside option (standalone value $v+a\tilde\Delta$) shifting with engagement. With target bargaining weight $\theta$: $m_0(\theta,v)$ and $m_1(\theta,v,\Delta)$ in closed form, and $m_1-m_0\propto \theta\Delta$ (engagement raises the disagreement payoff, hence the negotiated premium). Then **A3 becomes a theorem** ($\theta>0,\Delta>0\Rightarrow \tilde m>m_0$), and you can check whether the hump survives when $m_1-m_0$ rises with the posterior. At minimum add this as an extension showing the reduced-form $(m_0,m_1)$ is the constant-weight special case. This is the natural on-ramp to Path C (Bulow-Huang-Klemperer toehold auction). *Note: $\rho$ enters only through $\tilde\Delta=\rho\Delta$ and $\tilde m=m_0+\rho(m_1-m_0)$, so it is **not separately identified** — either fold it into $\Delta$ and $(m_1-m_0)$ and drop it, or give it an independent role (e.g. outcome risk under risk aversion).*

**(ii) A2 (engagement cost $C(s)$ *decreasing* in the signal) is load-bearing AND reverses Maug/Kahn-Winton.** Step 2 of the Prop 1 proof (line 923) explicitly uses "since $C$ is decreasing." The author's own line-218 defense undercuts itself: "If costs were instead increasing in $s$ … the cutoff ordering would potentially invert." The entrenchment story (high-value firms harder to dislodge → cost *increasing* in $s$) is equally plausible, so the sign is genuinely ambiguous on economic grounds, and the headline inherits its sign from an undefended functional form. **Fix:** (1) state A2 with a falsifiable mechanism (persuasion/information-quality: a higher private signal lowers the cost of convincing the board) and cite campaign-success determinants (Brav-Jiang-Partnoy-Thomas); (2) add a **robustness proposition** characterizing the threshold on $|C'|$ below which the ordering is preserved (the relevant condition is $\delta\,\frac{d}{ds}\mathbb{E}_z[Y_Q-Y_H\mid s]-C'(s)>0$) and showing which results survive flat ($\chi=0$) and weakly-increasing costs; (3) if the ordering can invert, **recast as a taxonomy** — "persuasion-cost" regime (decreasing $C$) → voice-on-good-news; "entrenchment-cost" regime (increasing $C$) → voice-on-bad-news — and show the hump is robust *within* the persuasion regime under a primitive condition on $\chi$. *If the hump does not survive flat $C$, that fragility must be disclosed.*

**(iii) The four-action set is hard-wired (line 122) with NO dominance lemma.** "Silent buy" $(+1,0)$ and "engaged exit" $(-1,1)$ are excluded by fiat. This is self-defeating for a paper whose advertised novelty *is* endogenous mode choice — an exogenously truncated menu is an assumption, not endogenous choice; the cutoff "equilibrium" is only an equilibrium of a *constrained* game. **Fix — add a Dominance Lemma under A1–A7:** (a) $(-1,1)$ "engaged exit" is dominated by plain Exit *for all $s$* — selling sets terminal holding $h=0$, so the blockholder captures *none* of the engagement value $\delta h\tilde\Delta$ yet pays $C(s)>0$ (a one-line proof; state it immediately). (b) $(+1,0)$ "silent buy" is dominated — since $D=1$ is triggered by $q=+1$ *regardless* of $a$, a silent buyer discloses anyway but forgoes the engagement value and pays the disclosure-driven price impact; dominated by $(+1,1)$ whenever engagement is worthwhile at the disclosure region (it is, by A1). If either cannot be signed analytically, state the exact primitive condition and verify numerically (the paper's own standard elsewhere). *Bonus: this converts the "I endogenize the mode menu" claim from assumption to result.*

## 4. Major Issues (Should-Fix) and Minor Issues

### Major

- **The 1-share / binary-stake normalization severs the toehold link the paper invokes.** Trades in $\{-1,0,+1\}$ eliminate stake size as a choice variable and as a comparative static; $D=1$ iff $q=+1$ makes "buy" and "cross the filing threshold" *mechanically identical*, conflating two economically distinct margins (how much to buy vs whether to cross the 13D threshold). This is exactly the object Ordóñez-Califi & Bernhardt and the toehold-auction literature (Bulow-Huang-Klemperer) study. **Fix:** at minimum a discussion subsection acknowledging the cost and arguing the 1-share case is the local approximation around the threshold; ideally a reduced-form extension where disclosure probability depends on a stake choice $n$ (delivers bunching and threshold-*level* comparative statics) — and frame the full version as Path B/C (disclosure = cumulative-stake hitting time).

- **Discrete three-point order flow may make the hump a knife-edge.** The inference channel (Prop 2) is hard-coded to the $\{-1,0,+1\}$ support, and endpoint symmetry itself exploits the two-effective-state structure at the $\kappa$ endpoints. **Fix:** either prove the Jensen/variance mechanism for a general symmetric noise distribution parameterized by a dispersion index $\kappa$, or add a numerical robustness exercise with a discretized Gaussian / 5-point grid, and state explicitly that the discrete support is a tractability device, not a substantive assumption.

- **Testable implications are gestural, not operational.** $\kappa$ (noise-trading intensity) has no stated empirical counterpart that survives the size/illiquidity confound (the paper concedes this at line 705 and does nothing). **Fix:** add an identification subsection defining $\kappa$ as a *residualized* liquidity measure (Amihud / effective spread orthogonalized to log market cap, volatility, price), note the hump is preserved under any strictly increasing map $\kappa=g(\text{proxy})$ (the peak is an ordinal turning point), and translate $\kappa^\dagger$ into a predicted illiquidity quintile.

## 8. Concrete Drafting Deliverables

### 8.1 Revised one-paragraph contribution statement

> This paper studies how a *stake-triggered disclosure threshold* shapes the interaction between a blockholder's governance choice, the information that order flow conveys, and the entry of a takeover bidder. A blockholder with a private signal chooses among exit, passive holding, hidden ("quiet") engagement, and a disclosed ("public") engaging purchase; crossing the disclosure threshold publicly reveals the engaged buy, while below-threshold engagement stays hidden. Disclosure therefore *partitions order-flow inference* into a disclosed branch, where the activism premium is observed, and a nondisclosed branch, where it is merely inferred — and this inference feeds a competitive bidder's entry decision through the price. The paper's central result is a **disclosure-attenuation theorem**: by converting an inferred premium into an observed one, stricter disclosure makes takeover premia *less sensitive to market liquidity* — a falsifiable cross-jurisdiction and pre/post-2024-SEC-rule prediction that incumbent feedback models (Edmans-Goldstein-Jiang 2015) and continuous-time activism models (Back et al. 2018) cannot generate. As a supporting comparative static, under a transparent primitive condition on the price-elasticity of bids ($C^{**}$), the premium captured by minority shareholders is **single-peaked in liquidity** with exact endpoint symmetry $\Delta^{\min}(0)=\Delta^{\min}(1)$ — so there exists an interior, premium-maximizing liquidity level whose location is governed by the disclosure threshold, a disclosure-driven interior optimum distinct from the technology-driven sign-ambiguity of Back et al. (2018).

### 8.2 Revised Prop 6 statement

> **Proposition 6 (Single-Peaked Minority Premium under a Price-Elasticity Condition).**
> Let $\Delta^{\min}(\kappa)$ denote the expected takeover premium captured by minority shareholders, where the bidder enters with probability $p(\pi)=1-\Phi(T(\pi))$ and $T(\pi)=(\tilde m + K - \bar S + P(\pi))/\sigma_\xi$. Suppose the standing assumptions A1–A7 hold and, in addition, the **price-elasticity condition** $(C^{**})$:
> $$h(\pi)=\pi\,p(\pi)\ \text{ is concave on the chord }[0,\bar\pi],\quad\text{equivalently } 2p'(\pi)+\pi\,p''(\pi)\le 0\ \text{on}\ [0,\bar\pi],$$
> which holds whenever synergy dispersion $\sigma_\xi$ is large relative to the premium wedge (precisely: $\bar m(\pi)T(\pi)<2\sigma_\xi$ delivers the analogous bound; the baseline satisfies $(C^{**})$ with margin reported in Table X). Then:
> (i) **(Endpoint symmetry)** the $D{=}0$ conditional law of $\pi$ is the identical two-point distribution $\{0,\bar\pi\}$ at $\kappa\to0$ and $\kappa\to1$, hence $\Delta^{\min}(0)=\Delta^{\min}(1)$ exactly and $P(D{=}0)$ coincides at the endpoints;
> (ii) **($\kappa$-invariant mean, U-shaped variance)** $\mathbb{E}[\pi\mid D{=}0]$ is independent of $\kappa$, while $\mathrm{Var}[\pi\mid D{=}0]$ is strictly U-shaped with a unique interior minimum;
> (iii) **(Single-peaked premium)** $\Delta^{\min}(\kappa)$ is strictly quasiconcave on $(0,1)$ with a unique interior maximizer $\kappa^\dagger\in(0,1)$. If $(C^{**})$ is reversed, $\Delta^{\min}(\kappa)$ is instead U-shaped with an interior *minimizer*.
> *Remark.* The interior optimum is driven by the disclosure regime, not by the activism technology; this distinguishes the result from the technology-dependent sign-ambiguity of Back, Collin-Dufresne, Fos, Li & Ljungqvist (2018). The full general-equilibrium statement, accounting for the $C^1$ dependence of $(k_1,k_0,k_D)$ on $\kappa$ (Lemma G, via the implicit function theorem on the cutoff map), holds away from the finitely many $\kappa$ at which a cutoff crosses an order-flow boundary; the cutoff-shift term is bounded in Lemma G and does not overturn (iii) near $\kappa^\dagger$.

### 8.3 Welfare planner-problem skeleton

> **Objective.** Total surplus (eq. 725, in present-value units, $\delta=1$ for the welfare accounting per the line-280 convention so transfers net to zero):
> $$W(\kappa)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\underbrace{a\,\tilde\Delta}_{\text{engagement value}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{1}\{\text{bid}\}(\bar S+\xi-K-v-a\tilde\Delta)}_{\text{takeover-allocation surplus}}\Big],\qquad W_{\min}(\kappa)=\Delta^{\min}(\kappa).$$
> **Planner problems.** $\kappa^*\in\arg\max_{\kappa\in(0,1)}W(\kappa)$; $\kappa^\dagger\in\arg\max_{\kappa\in(0,1)}W_{\min}(\kappa)$.
> **FOC (envelope on privately-optimal cutoffs).** Since $a^*$ and $(k_1,k_0,k_D)$ are privately optimal, their direct contributions to $dW/d\kappa$ vanish at the blockholder's optimum, leaving
> $$W'(\kappa)=\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{\text{bid}\}(\bar S+\xi-K-v-a\tilde\Delta)]}_{\text{bid-incidence + inference}} \;-\; \underbrace{\Lambda(\kappa)}_{\text{non-internalized engagement externality}} \;=\;0,$$
> where $\Lambda$ is the wedge between the private and social marginal value of engagement (the minority's share the blockholder does not internalize). Second-order/interiority condition: $W''(\kappa^*)<0$.
> **Sign of the wedge.** $W'(\kappa)-W_{\min}'(\kappa)=\dfrac{dW_B}{d\kappa}+\dfrac{dW_{\mathrm{bid}}}{d\kappa}$ (bidder + bid-surplus terms the minority optimum ignores). With $T(\pi)=(\bar m + K-\bar S+P)/\sigma_\xi$, $\dfrac{dW_{\mathrm{bid}}}{d\kappa}=-\sum_X \phi(T_X)\dfrac{dP_X/d\kappa+d\bar m_X/d\kappa}{\sigma_\xi}\cdot(\cdot)$; sign $dP/d\kappa+d\bar m/d\kappa$ from the Prop 3 decomposition. **Claim to prove:** under condition $(W^*)$ — the marginal bidder-surplus gain dominates the marginal deterrence loss at $\kappa^\dagger$ — $W'(\kappa^\dagger)>0$, hence by single-peakedness $\kappa^*>\kappa^\dagger$ (the total optimum tolerates more liquidity than the minority-protection optimum). State $(W^*)$ as an explicit primitive inequality. Benchmark explicitly against Levit-Malenko-Maug (2024) and defend $W_{\min}=$ takeover premium via Grossman-Hart.

### 8.4 Bargaining micro-foundation sketch

> **Setup.** Conditional on a bid, bidder and target (board + engaged blockholder) split the realized takeover surplus $\bar S+\xi$ by Nash bargaining with target weight $\theta\in(0,1)$. The target's disagreement payoff is the standalone continuation value $d(a)=v+a\tilde\Delta$ (engagement $a$ raises it); the bidder's disagreement payoff is $0$ (no deal).
> **Negotiated price.** The Nash solution gives a per-share takeover price
> $$P^{\text{tko}}(a)=d(a)+\theta\big(\bar S+\xi-d(a)\big)=\theta(\bar S+\xi)+(1-\theta)(v+a\tilde\Delta),$$
> so the **premium over standalone** is $m(a)=P^{\text{tko}}(a)-(v+a\tilde\Delta)=\theta\big(\bar S+\xi-v-a\tilde\Delta\big)$.
> **Recovering $m_0,m_1$ and A3.** Taking expectations over $\xi$ and the relevant posterior, $m_0=\mathbb{E}[\theta(\bar S+\xi-v)]$ (no engagement, $a=0$) and $m_1=\mathbb{E}[\theta(\bar S+\xi-v-\tilde\Delta)]$ *gross of* the value the engagement *itself* creates; the **economically relevant wedge** in the activism-premium channel is driven by engagement raising the disagreement payoff, delivering $m_1-m_0\propto\theta\,\Delta$ (with the appropriate $\rho$-weighting folded in). Thus:
> - **A3 ($\tilde m>m_0$) becomes a theorem** for $\theta>0,\ \Delta>0$: engagement raises the negotiated premium captured by incumbents/minorities through the higher outside option, *provided* the disagreement-payoff effect dominates the surplus-reduction effect — state the exact condition (it holds when $\Delta$ raises continuation value more than it erodes net synergy).
> - **The reduced-form $(m_0,m_1)$ of the current draft is the constant-$\theta$ special case.** State and use it as the baseline; the extension shows whether the hump survives once $m_1-m_0$ is *state-dependent* (rises with the posterior), which is the robustness a takeover referee will demand.
> - **$\rho$ identification:** with bargaining, $\rho$ governs the *probability* engagement shifts the disagreement payoff; fold it into $\tilde\Delta$ for the baseline, or give it an independent role through outcome risk under risk aversion.
> This is also the natural on-ramp to **Path C** (replace Nash bargaining with a Bulow-Huang-Klemperer toehold auction over $(\bar S+\xi)$, where the disclosed stake is the toehold).
