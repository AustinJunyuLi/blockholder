## The record fact that changes the picture

Before the ranking, one reading of the committed record that neither the pack nor GPT Pro used. The paper measures liquidity through an absolute value, S = |∂κ Δact|, and the pack describes the pooled premium as "a flat plateau that falls at high liquidity". The record says the opposite. In `numerical_v4/checks/t2_t1_check.json`, `kappa_profiles`, M_P rises monotonically across all 71 κ nodes at every one of the ten nodes: by a factor 1.15 (T=5, q0.1) to 1.87 (T=5, q0.9) and 2.24 at every T=10 node (0.0530 to 0.1187 premium points). Δact rises with it. In `t2_threshold_revelation_check.json` the coefficient lists have a positive top coefficient and negative coefficients below it, so ∂κ M_P is negative below the root and positive above; and `W_rev_tau`, `W_rev_tau_prime` are negative at all 71 κ for all eight pairs. The one-crossing lemma of memo 2 therefore certifies, from the record's coefficients, that M_P is strictly increasing on (κ*, 1) at every node, with κ* at most 0.146. This is not a 3 percent effect hidden inside a derivative. It is a first-order level fact, and its sign is the economics the absolute value threw away.

Two exact identities make that fact readable, and both are one-line proofs on the existing model. First, since p(I) is a function of the public information set (`numerical_v4/pooled.py`, `bid_probability`) and π = E[a | I], the tower property gives E[πp] = E[ap], so

Δact = Δm · P(a=1) · E[p | a=1],

and P(a=1) = P(Voice) is free of κ and of the rule at fixed policies. Every liquidity and rule effect in the paper runs through one object: how often a bidder enters against a blockholder who is in fact engaged. Second, by Lemma g1, only Voice plans carry marks (`menu.py`: gamma_bar = 1e-6 sits below every positive increment, Exit and Hold mark zero), so a single revealed building round sets π = 1 exactly. On the tape an engaged blockholder is either fully detected as engaged or silent, and a type with n building rounds is silent with probability exactly (κ/2)^n. The record then reads: E[p | Voice, pooled] = M_P/(Δm π̄_P) runs from 0.023 to 0.052 across the grid at T=10, against E[p | flagged] = M_F/Δm = 0.0115 at the median node (Δm = 0.2 from `params.py`). Bidder entry against an engaged blockholder is two to four and a half times higher in the pool than after a filing, and it rises with liquidity because liquidity keeps her silent.

The third fact is structural and is what makes "who gets caught" literal. In the calibrated menu the accumulation length n(s) is weakly decreasing in the signal and b*(s) is increasing, so the crossing date is decreasing in s and the flagged set is an upper set in s. The blockholders the rule catches are the fast builders, who place the fewest orders and are therefore exactly the ones the tape most often misses (silent with probability (κ/2)^1 or (κ/2)^2, which is 0.43 and 0.18 at κ = 0.85). The slow builders place many orders, are almost never silent, and cross late or never, so the rule never reaches them. The rule and the tape catch opposite blockholders. That is why the clock's effect on the premium level grows from 0.3 to 1 percent at κ = 0.15 to 18 to 54 percent at κ = 0.85 across the ladder (from `kappa_profiles`), and why the clock's composition ratio in `t5_who_gets_caught.json` collapses to 0.013 where the rule catches 91 percent of engaged blockholders and stays at 0.64 where it catches 10 percent.
